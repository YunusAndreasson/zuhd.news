United States — Southern Hemisphere conifer plantations grow up to 4 times faster.

That makes them candidates for carbon sinks, as afforestation pushes to plant trees where none grew.

The 192-site study in Nature found the trees skip the usual growth-versus-stress trade-off, carrying more sugar and starch reserves.

Alex Fajardo of the University of Talca said plantations carry "high biodiversity and social costs."

Researchers say the exact cause of the drought resilience needs more study.
