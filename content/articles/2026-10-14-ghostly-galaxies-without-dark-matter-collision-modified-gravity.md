New Haven — 3 galaxies, 65 million light-years away, lack dark matter.

Yale astrophysicist Michael Keim says they show dark matter is more than modified gravity.

A June paper proposes two galaxies collided, clumping their gas while dark matter passed through and left DF2, DF4 and DF9 bare.

"The absence of dark matter is, in a way, some evidence of dark matter, which is ironic," said Shyam Balaji, a King's College London theorist.

Candidates still range from stealthy neutrinos to WIMPs to atom-sized primordial black holes.
