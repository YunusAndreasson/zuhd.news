Washington State — Sudden heat shock raised honey bee deaths and altered gut microbes.

Bees reared in sustained heat adapted, while a sudden jump caused the damage.

Washington State University researchers, writing in Scientific Reports, kept 150 bees per enclosure at steady, constantly high or suddenly raised temperatures.

Lead author Priya Chakrabarti Basu says hormesis may explain it, but the gut-pesticide link needs further research.

Basu says solitary bees, which lack a colony's buffer, may fare worse in the wild.
