Shanghai — Chinese buyers paid a record $200-plus a ton above London copper prices.

Copper stood at $14,470 a ton in September, and importers are now paying more to secure supply.

Trafigura estimates data centres add 350,000 tonnes of demand this year, against about 28 million tonnes globally.

"We think there will be surpluses for the next few years," said Alice Fox, a Macquarie analyst.

Removing the [US](country:US) tariff threat could trigger "rapid liquidation" of warehoused copper, Macquarie says.
