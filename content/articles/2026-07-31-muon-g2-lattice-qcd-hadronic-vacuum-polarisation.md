Batavia — Theory moved, not the measurement.

A 25-year gap between predicted and measured muon magnetism is closing because supercomputers now calculate one hard term directly, replacing estimates drawn from older collision data — which those datasets now contradict.

Which collision experiments erred, not new particles, is physics' next open question.
