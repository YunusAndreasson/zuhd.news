Leiden — A magnet under 1 millimetre wide could detect dark matter.

Detectors built for heavy particles have steadily ruled out candidates, so physicists now chase far lighter ones.

Ultralight particles behave like waves, and a passing wave would amplify the levitating magnet's natural sway.

The Lux-Zeplin experiment reported an energetic collision in September, but many physicists doubt a WIMP caused it.

Nuclear atomic clocks, atom clouds in superposition and Earth's geomagnetic field are running the same hunt.
