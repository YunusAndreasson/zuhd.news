5,200 holes line a Peruvian hillside for 1.5 km. Researchers at the University of Sydney found the holes share the structure of a khipu, the knotted-string device used for Andean accounting, suggesting Monte Sierpe served as a stone-built economic ledger around 1350 CE. Which goods the holes recorded remains unknown.
