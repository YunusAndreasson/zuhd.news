Global — Shredding lithium batteries can release carbon nanotubes above the recommended exposure limit.

Nanotubes in nanomaterial-containing batteries raise occupational health concerns for recycling workers, the authors said.

Shredding to under 1 cm and milling to under 0.5 mm released both ultrafine particles and nanotubes.

Total particle counts stayed under 1,000 per cm³, so counting particles alone missed the nanotube exposure, the authors found.

The study is laboratory work on LiFePO₄ cells, and its authors call for method validation in industrial recycling.
