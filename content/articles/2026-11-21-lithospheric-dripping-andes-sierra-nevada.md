Sierra Nevada — Rock beneath mountains can sink hundreds of kilometres.

Mountain belts are shaped by rock sinking as well as rock pushed upward.

Dense rock at the base of the crust becomes unstable, breaks away and drags part of the rigid lithosphere down with it.

Seismic images only suggest drips occurred beneath the central Andes and California's Sierra Nevada.
