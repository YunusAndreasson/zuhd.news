London — Leukemia cells lacking TP53 blunt immune-drug attacks chemically.

TP53-deficient cells secrete TGF-β1, which suppresses T-cell proliferation and disables the immune attack before it begins.

TP53 mutations affect the hardest-to-treat acute myeloid leukemia cases; TGF-β1 blockade may restore drug response, researchers said.
