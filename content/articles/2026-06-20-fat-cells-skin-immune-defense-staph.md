London — Skin fat cells fight Staph aureus infections.

Adipocytes release a cathelicidin peptide; neutrophils convert it into antibacterial fragments — but obesity impairs this response despite thicker fat.

This explains higher drug-resistant staph risk in obese patients and suggests therapies targeting the peptide-neutrophil pathway.
