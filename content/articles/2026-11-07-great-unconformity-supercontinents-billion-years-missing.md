Wollongong — Earth deleted more than a billion years of its rocks.

Sandstone from 538 million years ago often sits directly on rock 1.2 to 1.8 billion years old.

Modellers at [Australia](country:AU)'s University of Wollongong say supercontinent breakup pushed landmasses upward through mantle motion, and erosion sanded them down.

Rival explanations, Snowball Earth glaciers among them, remain untested against global datasets, the authors say.

Researchers still debate the cause, though all agree the same forces set the stage for complex life.
