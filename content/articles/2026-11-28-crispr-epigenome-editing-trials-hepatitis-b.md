Paris — Epigenome editing cut hepatitis B activity 97% to 99% at high doses.

240 million people had hepatitis B in 2024, and no treatment reliably clears it.

A disabled Cas9 carries methylation enzymes that switch viral DNA off without cutting it, avoiding chromosomal errors.

Researchers have not yet shown the effect prevents liver damage or cancer.

Only the liver is reachable today, and Jamie Hackett says 90% of targets sit in the brain.
