New York — A 200-year-old theory of fluids is being replaced.

It missed things like quark-gluon plasma and active matter that still behave as fluids.

Physicists now build fluids bottom-up from surviving symmetries, rather than top-down from continuous matter obeying conservation laws.

Researchers are still testing the reformulation against superfluids and other known systems.
