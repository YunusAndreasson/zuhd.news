Berlin — [Germany](country:DE) gets the European Central Bank's economist seat, [Spain](country:ES) its presidency.

A Franco-German deal struck in Dublin last week settles who succeeds Christine Lagarde.

Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil proposed Princeton's Markus Brunnermeier to replace Philip Lane, whose term as chief economist ends in May.

The nomination ends a standoff in which Klingbeil wanted a woman as Germany's nominee.

Germany's cabinet votes Wednesday, and Paris would settle for Isabel Schnabel's markets seat, with Laurence Boone as candidate.
