Botswana — A 6.5 earthquake hit crust thought too rigid to tear.

The East African Rift's southern end reaches nearly 320 kilometres farther than thought, into the craton itself.

Researchers trenched the 80-kilometre Khurutshe Fault scarp, where land on one side sits up to 120 metres higher.

"It basically challenges age-old thinking," said Folarin Kolawole, a Lamont structural geologist and study co-author.

Whether rifts break through cratons remains an open question, lead author Boniface Kgosidintsi says.
