London — Copper's 9% weekly drop was its third-biggest in a decade.

Investors are unwinding bets that tied copper to data-centre growth, Critical Metals' chief executive says.

Saxo Bank's Ole Hansen says the Democratic sweep of [US](country:US) midterms raised doubts over AI and data-centre spending.

Copper briefly broke below $12,000 a tonne Monday, then recovered to $12,160.

Traders now watch Federal Reserve signals, with expected rate rises weighing on the metal.
