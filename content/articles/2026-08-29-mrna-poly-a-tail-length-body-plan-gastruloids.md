London — A messenger RNA's tail length shapes body plans.

It reveals a hidden layer of embryonic timing control missed by transcription alone.

Tail length sets how long a transcript survives and how readily it gets translated, acting as a developmental timer.

Researchers tested the mechanism in gastruloids, lab-grown models of an early mammalian body axis.
