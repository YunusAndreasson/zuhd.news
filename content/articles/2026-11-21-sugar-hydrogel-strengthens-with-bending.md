St. Louis — A sugar-based gel got stronger after 10,000 bends.

Chenfeng Ke expects orthopedic device makers to want it for artificial joints and tissue repair.

Stretching breaks large clusters of cyclodextrin rings, which slide along polymer chains and reorganize into smaller crystalline structures.

"The material gets stronger only where it's needed," said Ke, a Washington University chemist.

Ke's team has made only small quantities and is seeking medical partners and investors.
