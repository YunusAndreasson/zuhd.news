Hong Kong — Damaged cell compartments trap antisense drugs inside solid-like condensates.

Most antisense drugs that enter cells stay inside endolysosomes and never reach their targets.

When endolysosomes are damaged, proteins form dense condensates that hold the drugs in place despite the breach.

Making membranes more permeable raised drug activity, but most signal stayed inside.

University of Hong Kong researchers say the finding, in Nature Cell Biology, could guide delivery design.
