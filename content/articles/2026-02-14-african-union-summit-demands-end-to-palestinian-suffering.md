The African Union called for an end to the "extermination" of Palestinians and demanded the lifting of Israel's blockade on humanitarian goods at its 39th annual summit in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. AU Commission Chairman Mahmoud Ali Youssouf also urged the "silencing of the guns" across the continent, naming Sudan, the Sahel, eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, and Somalia as areas of concern.

## Why it matters

The summit is the continent's largest diplomatic gathering, representing approximately 1.4 billion people. Its explicit condemnation of Israeli operations in Gaza, where at least 72,045 people have been killed and 171,686 wounded since October 2023, signals continued pressure from the Global South on the conflict. The meeting also comes as Africa faces a wave of military takeovers in West African nations including Mali, Burkina Faso, Guinea, Niger, and Guinea-Bissau.

## The details

- AU Commission Chairman Youssouf said "international law and international humanitarian law are the basis of the international community" and demanded an end to the blockade on humanitarian goods to Palestinian territory
- UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called for permanent African representation on the UN Security Council, saying "this is 2026, not 1946"
- Water sanitation was the summit's central theme, intersecting with disputes including the Egypt-Ethiopia tensions over the Nile and farmer-herder conflicts in Nigeria
- The continent is home to roughly 400 million people aged 15 to 35 but paradoxically has some of the world's oldest and longest-serving leaders
- Observers noted nations at the summit signaling shifting geopolitical alliances, with several countries moving closer to China

## What's next

The summit's declarations will be tested against realities on the ground, particularly in Sudan, where civil war continues, and in eastern Congo, where conflict has intensified. The call for UN Security Council reform adds to growing momentum from African and Global South nations for a restructuring of international institutions, though any change requires approval from the current permanent members.
