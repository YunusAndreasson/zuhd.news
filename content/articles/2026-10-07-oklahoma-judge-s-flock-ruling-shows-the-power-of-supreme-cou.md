Oklahoma — A federal judge called a Flock search "indiscriminate mass surveillance."

It extends June's Supreme Court Chatrie ruling on phone location histories to roadside cameras.

Judge Sara Hill ruled a deputy's warrantless Flock search, showing 50 sightings of Melisa Kyle's car in a month, was unconstitutional.

The decision sets no binding precedent, but breaks with courts that allowed warrantless plate searches.

Experts say Hill's finding will influence judges in future cases involving plate-reader data.
