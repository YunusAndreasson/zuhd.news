Neuchâtel — 32 speakers used different tongue movements to produce identical vowels.

The authors propose such variation could seed differences among the world's 7,000 or so languages.

A PLOS Biology study ran real-time magnetic resonance imaging on 16 men and 16 women saying 5 American English vowels.

The authors caution the sample was small and compared only a few language families.

They offer the variation as a baseline for testing which social or historical factors drove language diversification.
