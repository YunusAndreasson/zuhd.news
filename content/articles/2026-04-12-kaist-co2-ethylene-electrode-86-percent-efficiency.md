Daejeon — KAIST hit 86% Faradaic efficiency converting CO₂ to ethylene. A new electrode blocks water penetration without impeding electrons or CO₂—solving the flooding problem limiting industrial-scale conversion. Ethylene, made entirely from fossil fuels at 200 million tons per year, is the target this unlocks.
