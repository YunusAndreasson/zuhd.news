Nikhil Gupta, a 54-year-old Indian national, pleaded guilty in a US federal court to charges of murder-for-hire, conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire, and money laundering in connection with a 2023 plot to assassinate Sikh separatist leader Gurpatwant Singh Pannun on American soil. Gupta faces 20 to 24 years in prison under federal sentencing guidelines, with sentencing scheduled for May 29.

## Why it matters

The case has strained diplomatic relations between the United States, Canada, and India. US prosecutors allege an Indian intelligence officer, Vikash Yadav, directed the operation, an assertion India denies. The guilty plea marks a significant development in what American and Canadian authorities describe as a broader campaign of transnational repression targeting Indian dissidents abroad, raising questions about the limits of state-sponsored operations on foreign soil.

## The details

- Gupta transferred $15,000 from India in 2023 to a person he believed would carry out the killing, but who was actually a confidential source for the US Drug Enforcement Administration
- Prosecutors say Indian intelligence officer Vikash Yadav recruited Gupta in May 2023 to arrange the assassination
- Gupta was detained at Prague airport in the Czech Republic in June 2023 and extradited to the United States
- Pannun, who holds dual US-Canadian citizenship, leads Sikhs for Justice, a New York-based organization advocating for Punjab's independence
- Indian officials have denied government involvement, saying such operations contradict their policy

## What's next

Gupta's sentencing on May 29 will determine the final prison term within the 20-to-24-year range. The target of the plot, Pannun, has called on US authorities to pursue those in India who he says authorized the operation, describing Gupta as "just a foot soldier." The case is likely to remain a point of friction in US-India relations, particularly as Washington weighs its strategic partnership with New Delhi against concerns over transnational repression.
