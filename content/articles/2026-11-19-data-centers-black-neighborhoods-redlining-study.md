United States — Majority-Black neighborhoods have 2.2 times the odds of hosting a data center.

The odds reach 2.6 in majority-Hispanic and 4.2 in majority-Asian neighborhoods, researchers found.

Researchers analyzed 81,000 census tracts and 5,291 data centers, and the gaps persisted after controlling for income, land costs and power-line access.

"It’s just how embedded race is with all these other factors," said Dhanaraj Thakur, a study co-author.

A data center is slated for Taylor, Texas land donated for a park beside a Black neighborhood.
