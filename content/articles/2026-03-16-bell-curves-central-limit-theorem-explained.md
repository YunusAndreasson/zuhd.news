Bell curves started as a trick for 18th-century gamblers. Abraham de Moivre proved that combining enough random outcomes always converges to a bell curve — the theorem modern statistics depends on. Carnegie Mellon statistician Larry Wasserman warns that most practitioners never test whether the theorem's assumptions hold.
