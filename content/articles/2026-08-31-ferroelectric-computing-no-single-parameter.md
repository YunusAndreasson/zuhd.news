London — No single material wins the race to replace charge-based memory.

The tradeoffs decide what device you get: dense storage, low-power updates, or fast switching.

Ferroelectric materials could give non-volatile memory without today's write-energy cost, but each property trades against the others.

Researchers say pick materials by application, not one universal candidate.
