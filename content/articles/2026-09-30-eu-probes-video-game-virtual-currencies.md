Brussels — 9 video game companies face EU consumer investigations.

EU guidelines require games to show real-world prices for virtual currencies, and the EU consumer chief cites risks to children.

A network of 27 states' authorities is coordinating after last year's talks produced no satisfactory results, the Commission said.

Authorities will now test whether Ubisoft, Mojang, Riot Games and Supercell breached EU consumer law.
