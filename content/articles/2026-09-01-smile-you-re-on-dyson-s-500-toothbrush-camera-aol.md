Singapore — Dyson's $500 toothbrush watches your teeth.

It joins doorbells, vacuums and fridges normalizing cameras inside private spaces.

A 100k-pixel lens shoots 28 images a second, detecting gaps to target with a precision mouthrinse jet.

Dyson says images aren't stored or shared, but hasn't detailed its retention or security practices.
