Jerusalem — Netanyahu's 3-page doctrine makes preemptive war official policy.

It also licenses buffer zones along [Israel](country:IL)'s borders, without saying where.

Published 9 days before the 27 October election, it names Iran, Hezbollah and the Houthis as threats.

Gadi Eisenkot, Netanyahu's main challenger, called it "two hollow pages, full of slogans that have nothing to do with a security concept."

Israel votes 27 October, and Polymarket traders give Netanyahu a 28% chance of remaining prime minister, down 5 points in a week.
