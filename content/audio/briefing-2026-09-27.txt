Iran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days... and President Trump turned it down. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi's plan asked Washington to lift its port blockade, waive oil sanctions and free twelve billion dollars, terms that would have eased a seven-month war's grip on the world's most vital oil route. Instead, Tehran's tightening its hold another way: the state-affiliated Mehr agency says Iran's new strait authority will blacklist charterers who send ships off its approved lanes.

In politics. <short pause>
Hamas's leader, Khalil al-Hayya, offered Mahmoud Abbas unity talks on Saturday, and said no Israeli election result will change conditions for Palestinians. Palestinians are scheduled to vote on the twenty-eighth of November, but Abbas requires every candidate to accept the platform of the Palestine Liberation Organisation, which has recognised Israel since nineteen ninety-three... a rule that could bar Hamas entirely. The split between the two movements has divided Palestinian governance since two thousand seven, and whether this offer bridges it may decide who speaks for Gaza.

Swiss voters decide today whether to write strict neutrality into their constitution. A Yes would bar defence cooperation with military alliances unless Switzerland is attacked, and would block sanctions the UN hasn't approved, curbing Bern's adoption of EU measures against Russia. Neutrality's been Swiss doctrine since the Congress of Vienna in eighteen fifteen, and the Swiss People's Party is now using direct democracy to reverse the government's drift toward NATO since twenty twenty-two.

The United Naga Council wants New Delhi to suspend its ceasefire pact with Kuki armed groups, which it accuses of taking part in the violence. Three years into Manipur's ethnic conflict, a truce meant to calm one community has become another community's grievance.

Cuba's foreign minister, Bruno Rodríguez, told the General Assembly that Washington's oil blockade is collective punishment, designed to engineer a humanitarian crisis. On top of an embargo that's stood since nineteen sixty-two, this year's blockade has left eleven million Cubans with fuel shortages and blackouts, and when fuel stops, so do water pumps, refrigeration and hospitals. Trump says a deal is coming without military action, but he hasn't said what Havana would have to give up.

An eyewall replacement has weakened it slightly, but communities in Baja California Sur, some still marked by Hurricane Odile in twenty fourteen, are bracing with Hurricane Odalys close behind.

On the economy. <short pause>
A thirty percent cut in malaria funding could add one hundred forty-six million cases across Africa by twenty thirty, according to the African Leaders Malaria Alliance. The alliance says it would also cause nearly four hundred thousand deaths and thirty-seven billion dollars in lost output, on a continent that already carries ninety-four percent of the world's cases, most of them in young children. Leaders want African treasuries to fill the gap after the Global Fund and Gavi missed their fundraising targets, but those budgets are already strained by an oil shock Africa didn't cause.

President Trump announced he's rolling back Biden's fuel economy target of fifty point four miles per gallon for twenty thirty-one. The timing is striking: Brent crude reached a hundred and fourteen dollars ninety a barrel on the twenty-second of September, up nearly twenty-four percent in a month, largely because of Washington's own war with Iran. Federal standards make automakers balance efficient cars against inefficient ones across their fleets, so loosening them moves the cost onto drivers at the pump and weakens the push toward electric vehicles.

In science. <short pause>
Hurricane Polo's jump from tropical storm to Category Five in a single day has exposed a blind spot in AI weather models, which now rival physics-based forecasts on storm tracks but still miss rapid intensification. They learn from observations that are sparse over the open ocean, where hurricanes strengthen, and training on historical records tends to smooth out the rare extremes that matter most. That's exactly the forecast coastal communities can't do without... because a storm that strengthens overnight can leave too little time to evacuate.

Two studies in Science Advances suggest Saturn's moon Enceladus may host life that a passing probe could detect. In one, a microbe from Earth's deep-sea vents did better than expected in a simulated Enceladus ocean, and in the other, ocean droplets freezing slowly in space sorted organic molecules into separate grains, concentrating any signs of life. NASA's Cassini spacecraft flew through the moon's icy plumes before its mission ended in twenty seventeen, yet no probe built for that search is on its way.

Russian state news agency TASS, citing Axios, reports that OpenAI has paused training its most advanced models. According to the report, staff at OpenAI and Anthropic are investigating tens of thousands of incidents from testing and deployment, and an OpenAI representative said training will resume only once extra safeguards are in place. If it's confirmed, it'd be the first time a frontier lab has halted training on safety grounds, and a test of whether such a pause can hold under commercial pressure.

It's the same aircraft whose flight-control software was implicated in the Lion Air and Ethiopian Airlines crashes that killed three hundred forty-six people, and airlines around the world are flying with a workaround until that fix arrives.
