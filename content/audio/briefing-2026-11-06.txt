More than fifty thousand people have poured into Taiz in just a few weeks... a city that's itself lived under siege for a decade. Fighting between government forces and Ansarallah has now reached the villages on its edge, after Ansarallah took Al-Turbah, seventy kilometres to the south, and coalition airstrikes followed. One mother of four fled the city, then came back, a sign of how little safe ground is left in Yemen's southwest.

In politics. <short pause>
Drones have killed at least nineteen people at a camp of Colombia's National Liberation Army in Venezuela's Apure state. It's Caracas's first major attack on a guerrilla group that Chavista governments have sheltered for two decades, and the strike reportedly came with American help, after Washington spent months pressing Delcy Rodríguez's government to end that protection. Border communities in Apure and Arauca now sit between a guerrilla force under fire and two states rewriting their terms.
<long pause>
Sixty-seven of the Knesset's hundred and twenty members have recommended Gadi Eisenkot for prime minister, against forty-six for Benjamin Netanyahu. Arab-led parties, holding a record nineteen seats, supplied that majority, because neither Netanyahu's bloc of fifty-three nor Eisenkot's Jewish-led parties with forty-eight can reach sixty-one on their own. Yet Eisenkot rules out governing with the very lists that lifted him, and President Isaac Herzog names his nominee on Sunday.
<long pause>
Congo's Ebola outbreak has now killed more than five thousand one hundred people, according to UN figures. This is the Bundibugyo strain, which spreads through bodily fluids rather than the air, and only once symptoms begin... and this week a British doctor who caught it treating patients was flown to isolation in London. Congolese nurses in North Kivu, where armed groups obstruct vaccination and contact tracing, don't have that way out.
<long pause>
Honduras has recognized Próspera, a private charter city on the island of Roatán, as a political subdivision — two years after its own courts banned such zones. The Winklevoss brothers, who gave more than forty-six million dollars to Donald Trump and his agenda, back Infinita, a firm that's operated inside the enclave. Economic Development Secretary Eddy Ordóñez signed the recognition, and it isn't yet clear whether Honduran judges will get the last word over land that writes its own laws.
<long pause>
Nearly thirty European flights received bomb threats by the end of Thursday, after seven flights and a cruise ship were targeted on Tuesday. France and Bulgaria scrambled fighter jets to escort airliners, and passengers were taken off planes in Madrid, after an email warned of devices on flights from Helsinki and Dublin. No government has named a culprit, and no claim has been verified, though security experts see the hallmarks of hybrid sabotage.
<long pause>
Thomas Massie, a Republican congressman, says he'll force a House vote on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, who championed the war on Iran. His party now blames that war for Tuesday's midterm rout, and Vice President JD Vance says Donald Trump himself conceded it cost them Congress. Impeachment takes two hundred and eighteen House votes, but conviction needs sixty-seven senators... so at least eleven Republicans would have to break ranks.

On the economy. <short pause>
Paraguay's president, Santiago Peña, resigned on Thursday, just hours after regulators seized Ueno Bank, which claimed sixty percent of the country's adults as customers. Public money supplied about a quarter of its deposits, while the bank lent to its owners' own companies beyond legal limits. A lender that grew too big to fail also grew too big to oversee, and ordinary depositors are now waiting to learn how far insurance will reach.
<long pause>
Vladimir Putin has seized the Russian business of Japan Tobacco, maker of thirty-seven percent of the country's cigarettes, which earned a quarter of the company's operating profit last year. A decree hands four factories to a local firm's temporary administration, so Japan Tobacco still owns them but no longer runs them — the same tool Moscow has turned on companies from states it calls unfriendly. Tokyo wants the decree reversed, and every foreign firm that stayed in Russia is reading the precedent.
<long pause>
Egypt's Suez Canal earned seven hundred and twenty-four million dollars in October, nearly double a year earlier and its best month since Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping began. Ship transits rose by almost a third to one thousand four hundred and ninety, chairman Osama Rabie said, even as the war in Iran holds Gulf crude exports at eighty-six percent of pre-war levels. Meanwhile JP Morgan estimates the oil market has tipped into a surplus of one point four million barrels a day, as high prices crush demand... so the windfall's durability is far from certain.
<long pause>
LIV Golf is entering liquidation, closing out Saudi Arabia's five billion dollar bet on the sport. Seven executives collected fifteen point six million dollars before the September bankruptcy filing, and a three hundred million dollar rescue by the investment firm BC Partners fell apart. The kingdom's Public Investment Fund is trimming its giga-spending as oil revenue tightens, and LIV may not be the last Vision twenty thirty project to go.

In science. <short pause>
China's carbon dioxide emissions fell three point one percent in the third quarter, the sharpest drop since the lockdowns of twenty twenty-two. Solar, wind and nuclear added a hundred and ten terawatt hours, twice the growth in demand, while oil burned for transport fell nine percent as the Hormuz crisis squeezed supply. The world's largest emitter has been flat or falling since March twenty twenty-four — and whether this peak holds depends on how much of it is structure, and how much is war.
<long pause>
Traces of tetrahydrocannabinol, the compound behind the cannabis high, have survived in the hair of a person buried twenty-eight hundred years ago in southern Siberia. It's the earliest direct proof of psychoactive cannabis use, according to a study in the journal Science, from a royal mound ringed by human and horse sacrifices where excavators found more than fourteen hundred seeds. Herodotus wrote of Scythians breathing in hemp smoke... and now chemistry has caught up with the historian.
<long pause>
Just three point five percent of the world's ocean is effectively protected, experts told biodiversity talks in Yerevan, Armenia's capital. Donors pledged twenty billion dollars a year by twenty twenty-five, but gave eighteen point nine billion in twenty twenty-four, and ministers from the Global South say rich countries are holding back what they promised. Governments have four years left to meet their global protection target, and the money isn't keeping pace with the map.

In technology. <short pause>
A new filing in Cape Town confirms that Equinix, the American data-centre giant, is the force behind the data centres planned near the city's airport. Its partner, the developer Atterbury, had called the application an inconsequential two hundred and eighty square metre boundary correction, and the city rewrote its by-law to class data centres as warehouses after Equinix's lawyers warned it wouldn't invest otherwise. As African cities compete to host the cloud, the appeal asks who writes the rules when the investor holds the pen.
<long pause>
From today, owning pornography that depicts strangulation is a crime in England. The new offences also cover family role play and performers who are, or pretend to be, under sixteen, with up to three years for possession and five for publishing. Courts will now decide where that line falls, and free speech groups are watching how widely it's drawn.
