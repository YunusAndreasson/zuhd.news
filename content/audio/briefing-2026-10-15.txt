This is your briefing for the fifteenth of October, twenty twenty-six.
<long pause>
Brazil's Senate voted seventy-five to one to end the six-day working week... the largest change to Brazilian working hours since the nineteen eighty-eight constitution. More than thirty-seven million formal workers, by the government's count, gain a second weekly rest day, as the legal week falls from forty-four hours to forty without a cut in pay. It passed ten days before the runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro, and employers now have fourteen months to show what it really costs them.

In politics. <short pause>
In Deir al-Balah, a root canal now costs three hundred shekels and an extraction just fifty... so families living on aid are choosing to lose the tooth. Three years of Israeli attacks, and a siege that blocks anaesthetic and filling materials along with other medical supplies, have all but destroyed Gaza's dental care. A blockade isn't only measured in bombs, it turns out, but in treatable infections that end in pulled teeth.
<long pause>
Egypt and Sudan are negotiating a joint security framework for their eastern border, after a bombing on Saturday killed scores of Sudanese gold miners in the Halayeb Triangle. That triangle's been disputed since Britain drew two different lines there, in eighteen ninety-nine and nineteen-oh-two, and today Egypt administers land that Sudan still claims. On Monday, a gunman from an army-allied force killed two Egyptians in a Khartoum market... and whose security a pact sold as fighting organised crime will really serve remains unclear.
<long pause>
A Saturday strike on Riyadh's airport killed thirteen people, and analysts now say Saudi Arabia's bet on containing Yemen's Houthi movement has collapsed. Saudi air defences stop roughly nine in ten missiles, a Western defence official said, yet the Houthis still reached Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran, more than a decade after the kingdom went to war in Yemen in twenty fifteen. Riyadh now faces a choice between escalation and a deal with Sanaa, and Yemenis would pay for either.
<long pause>
A teaching hospital in Bannu, in Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, has ended the house-job training of every Afghan medical graduate with immediate effect. These are doctors trained in Pakistan, midway to a licence, and trainees at two other hospitals were dismissed and told to leave as well. Pakistan's campaign against Afghan residents has moved from deportations to closing professions, while Afghanistan, where the Taliban bar women from medical school, loses doctors it can't train itself.
<long pause>
Kenya has confirmed a second Ebola case... a thirty-nine-year-old relative of the first patient, who fell ill while already in quarantine. That's contact tracing working, and it's the only real defence, because the Bundibugyo strain, first identified in Uganda in two thousand seven, has no approved vaccine. Health officials have located one hundred and thirteen of one hundred and twenty-eight contacts, and the chain runs back to Congo, where the outbreak has passed nine thousand cases.
<long pause>
Six hundred thousand Ukrainians lost power overnight, despite the energy truce Donald Trump brokered. Kyiv says it paused its strikes on Russian refineries under the deal, while Russia fired thirty-nine ballistic missiles and more than a hundred and twenty drones at energy sites in Kyiv and Kharkiv. Zelensky says he feels betrayed... and a fifth winter of full-scale war is only weeks away.

On the economy. <short pause>
Two Emirati cargo ships sank in the Strait of Hormuz within twenty-four hours, making October one of the most violent months there since the war began. Iranian officials say the escalation's deliberate, meant to keep oil prices high before America's midterm elections on the third of November. Roughly a fifth of the world's oil normally moves through this channel, and the sailors aboard these ships are the first to pay for turning fuel prices into a campaign weapon.
<long pause>
India's cabinet has approved selling just over sixty percent of IDBI Bank to Canada's Fairfax Financial for fifty-two thousand, eight hundred and seventy-eight crore rupees. The government calls it the country's largest disinvestment, and it's the first time a foreign group will control a state-owned Indian bank, one the Life Insurance Corporation was drafted in to rescue. IDBI shares fell up to seven percent because the price undercut the market... and a lender of public deposits will now answer to a holding company in Toronto.
<long pause>
Nearly a quarter of Filipino families, twenty-two point six percent, went involuntarily hungry in September, according to Social Weather Stations. That's higher than the twenty twenty pandemic average of twenty-one point one percent, and it tracks inflation at a three-year high of seven point two percent, with rice up more than twenty percent. Because the Philippines relies so heavily on imported rice, whether hunger eases depends on prices that Manila doesn't fully control.

In science. <short pause>
Off Antalya, archaeologists in Türkiye have dated a shipwreck to roughly four and a half thousand years ago, the era of the Great Pyramid, making it the oldest known. The hull's long since rotted away, so Macquarie University's Karin Sowada dated it from the clays of more than twenty barnacled ceramic pieces. Further tests on those clays could reveal where the jars were made... and which Bronze Age ports were already trading across the sea.
<long pause>
NASA's DAVINCI mission will now launch to Venus in December twenty thirty, a year early, and should return the first direct images of its surface since nineteen eighty-two. Those last pictures came from the Soviet Venera landers, which survived the crushing heat and pressure for barely two hours. The money came from cancelling Mars Sample Return and dropping a radar contribution to Europe's EnVision mission, while the Trump administration has twice proposed deep cuts to NASA science.

In technology. <short pause>
Seven Chinese ministries have drafted a fifty-four-article rulebook for gig workers, the country's first departmental rule covering people managed by platforms without formal employment. Article twenty-nine requires a human, not an algorithm alone, to review account bans, order cutoffs and large compensation demands for delivery riders and drivers. The mainland outlet that reported it summed up the gap bluntly: flexible work that isn't flexible, and freelancing that isn't free.
<long pause>
In Oslo, a class action alleges Telenor handed Myanmar's military junta one thousand, two hundred and fifty-three phone numbers, along with call logs, locations, ID numbers and linked bank accounts. Among those affected was Phyo Zeya Thaw, a former lawmaker arrested right after the junta accessed his account, his widow says, and executed in twenty twenty-two. The case tests whether a company can answer in its home courts for what a junta does with the data it hands over.
<long pause>
Mexico has given Meta, TikTok and X one month to protect minors by design, restricting violent, harassing and sexual content and removing risky material from young users' profiles. President Claudia Sheinbaum's decree follows an attack by two eighteen-year-olds on their former high school in Coahuila. Unlike Australia's age ban, it puts the duty on how platforms are built... and whether three of the world's largest companies comply in thirty days is the open question.
