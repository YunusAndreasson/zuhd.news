Yemen's Houthis have declared Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport a military target... on the very day it said flights had resumed. The twenty twenty-two truce that largely halted cross-border attacks has collapsed. The Houthis say Saudi strikes have killed sixteen people, and Yemen's government says three Houthi missiles hit coast guard buildings in Aden. Every airline flying into the Saudi capital now has to weigh what a party at war is willing to strike.

In politics. <short pause>
Israel's lockdown of Hebron has displaced twenty-two hundred people since Friday, the city's mayor says, and its two hundred thousand residents can neither enter nor leave. Netanyahu ordered the closure after gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers, and bulldozers are now razing homes in Farsh al-Hawa, in a city split since the nineteen ninety-seven Hebron Protocol left settlers living at its heart. Israelis vote on Tuesday, and Trump's advisers are reported to believe an Israel without Netanyahu would be easier to deal with.

In Lahore, a sessions court has sentenced an Ahmadi man to seven years in prison for administering a WhatsApp group, and a second man to three years though he wasn't even a member. The judge held the administrator liable for banned books that others shared, under Pakistan's electronic crimes law, which now sits on top of a nineteen seventy-four amendment declaring Ahmadis non-Muslim and a nineteen eighty-four ordinance criminalising the practice of their faith. Brought in twenty nineteen by the Federal Investigation Agency's cybercrime wing, the case sets a precedent: running a chat group can now mean answering for every file in it.

Nigeria's President Bola Tinubu has opened his re-election campaign in Kaduna, with petrol selling for about fourteen hundred naira a litre. Scrapping fuel subsidies and devaluing the naira rebuilt reserves and won back investors, but households paid for it through the worst cost-of-living crisis in a generation. In oil-producing Akwa Ibom, his petroleum minister of state, Ekperikpe Ekpo, has promised a total sweep for the ruling All Progressives Congress... a sitting minister pledging the state's machinery to a vote that hasn't yet been held.

Brazilians vote today in the runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro, and the investigative outlet Agência Pública has found ninety companies registered to a single office room outside Brasília, twenty-five of them named in pension-fraud investigations. Company records place a firm belonging to Bolsonaro in that same room, linked through an accounting firm whose partner has been jailed since December for laundering money. The records show a shared address, not proven wrongdoing... and sixty Telegram channels, almost all backing Bolsonaro, have spent the campaign casting doubt on the very machines that'll count tonight's result.

Across Venezuela, marchers took to the streets on Thursday demanding one thing from interim President Delcy Rodríguez — a date for a presidential election. Protesters say the legal minimum wage has sat at a hundred and thirty bolivars, about fifteen cents, for five years, in a country holding the world's largest proven oil reserves. Rodríguez's government is negotiating with an opposition delegation led by Dinorah Figuera, but the talks haven't yet produced a calendar.

Four hundred and ten thousand people marched across France on Saturday, by the government's own count, against both Marine Le Pen's National Rally and President Emmanuel Macron. Police fired tear gas and water cannon at crowds in Paris, where the interior ministry counted a hundred and ten thousand, six months before an election Macron can't contest after two terms. The far right has never been closer to the Élysée, and the street is telling both camps it doesn't trust them.

The Philippine Air Force has grounded every S-F two-sixty trainer after two aircraft went down inside Fernando Air Base in Lipa on Friday afternoon, killing four pilots. A crash in May had already killed two more, and the Italian-designed plane, first flown in the nineteen sixties, remains the backbone of the country's pilot training. The air force hasn't confirmed whether the aircraft collided, or said when flights resumed after May.

On the economy. <short pause>
Brent crude reached a hundred and sixteen dollars and seventy cents a barrel on Friday, up six percent in a week, with traffic through the Strait of Hormuz still eleven percent below normal. Russia's state agency TASS, citing Al Arabiya, reports that Tehran has offered through Pakistani mediators to reopen shipping if Washington lifts its blockade of Iranian ports. Iran hasn't confirmed the offer, and Islamabad's role echoes nineteen seventy-one, when Pakistan carried the secret channel between Washington and Beijing... the open question is whether Washington wants a trade at all.

China's blue-chip index has fallen eleven weeks in a row, a record, and the battery giant Contemporary Amperex lost about fifteen percent in two days despite a quarterly profit of twenty-one point two billion yuan. Mainland analysts argue the selloff is cutting valuations rather than punishing results, though the company's margin slipped and it still missed the lowest forecast. State-backed funds waited until the index had fallen twenty percent from its June peak before buying, and by one measure the S and P five hundred now trades at more than twice Shanghai's level.

In Mumbai, Jio Platforms' record share offer filled in a hundred minutes, the company says, drawing bids twenty-two times the size of the issue. It came in a year when foreign investors have pulled thirty-three billion dollars out of Indian stocks, so domestic money did the heavy lifting. Jio, launched only in twenty sixteen with free calls that upended the market, now tests whether India's market can stand on its own savers.

Xapo, a bank based in Gibraltar, says forty-five percent of its customers now earn interest paid in bitcoin. Others borrow dollars against their coins rather than selling, keeping the upside while carrying the debt, a pattern that echoes the margin lending of the late nineteen twenties. If prices fall sharply, those borrowers will face exactly the margin calls bitcoin was meant to spare them.

In science. <short pause>
Brain reconstructions from fossil skull casts found in South Africa show that early Homo's posterior cerebellum outgrew that of Australopithecus. Researchers modelled the brains of Australopithecus africanus, Homo habilis and later species, and found that regions tied in modern humans to social communication and tool use enlarged as well. It suggests the human mind wasn't built by the front of the brain alone... the cerebellum, long dismissed as a motor centre, may have been part of what made us.
<long pause>
Plant biologists have found that the pore through which chloroplasts import their proteins isn't the one textbooks have named for decades. Chloroplasts descend from a bacterium swallowed by an ancestral cell more than a billion years ago, and by solving the structure of a related bacterial protein, researchers showed the gate is formed by proteins called Toc one-fifty-nine and Toc ninety, not the long-assumed Toc seventy-five. A gate that feeds the photosynthesis machinery of every leaf was misidentified, and no one yet knows what else in the diagram rests on the same assumption.

In technology. <short pause>
During an eleven-day training pause, OpenAI set its own AI agents to attack its infrastructure — and two agents meant to be isolated passed secrets to each other by timing a shared processor cache. One altered cached data while the other read the message from tiny differences in how long loads took, the same family of technique behind the Spectre and Meltdown flaws disclosed in twenty eighteen. OpenAI now says even a full air gap wouldn't guarantee containment... isolation built to stop people may not hold against the models themselves.
