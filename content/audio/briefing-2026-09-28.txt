Iranian media say explosions hit near Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz... just hours after President Trump rejected Tehran's proposal to reopen the waterway. Ships there must now follow a temporary route Iran has approved and pay fees, and charterers who send their crews around it are blacklisted. In politics. <short pause>
South Korea's demanding an apology from Volodymyr Zelensky, after he announced at the UN General Assembly that two captured North Korean soldiers were being handed over to Seoul. The transfer had been kept secret to protect the prisoners' families still living in the North, where relatives of defectors have long faced punishment under a system of guilt by association. Seoul's been one of Ukraine's quieter backers on arms supply... and that support is now strained just as South Korea is trying to reopen its own channels to Pyongyang.

At least sixty-two people have died after three days of rain flooded Uttar Pradesh, India's most populous state, and more than a thousand homes have been damaged. The rain came from a Bay of Bengal weather system at a time when the monsoon should already be withdrawing, and it fell hardest on rural families in mud-walled homes who have the least means to rebuild. Whether the state's relief can match what those families have lost is the question that'll stay after the water goes down.

In Kuala Lumpur, Siti Hasmah Mohamad Ali, Malaysia's former first lady, has died at the age of one hundred. She trained as one of the first Malay women doctors, and she led rural maternal and public-health programmes long before her husband became prime minister and throughout her decades as first lady. Her death leaves Mahathir Mohamad, now a hundred and one, as one of the last living members of the generation that shaped the country after independence.

Aleksandar Vucic resigned as Serbia's president on Sunday... so that he can run for prime minister in next month's elections. The constitution bars a third presidential term but says nothing against a return to the premiership, a post he's already held twice, and it's the same route Vladimir Putin took in Russia in two thousand eight. After fourteen years at the top, Serbians are left to ask whether a new title will change anything at all.

According to the Israeli broadcaster i-twenty-four News, citing unnamed sources, Benjamin Netanyahu has ordered officials to prepare an International Criminal Court case against Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan over the Kurds and Hamas. Netanyahu himself faces an arrest warrant from that court, whose authority Israel rejects, and since neither country is a member, a third state would have to refer the case, and the court would still need jurisdiction over the alleged crimes. A leader who denies the court's reach over himself is now reportedly reaching for it as a weapon against a NATO member.

In Sudan, drones from the Rapid Support Forces struck the Umm Alila fuel depot near Wad Madani at dawn, and fire crews contained the blaze to its tanks. The depot supplies petrol and diesel for pumps, bakeries and hospitals across Gezira, the farming heartland the army took back from the paramilitary in twenty twenty-five. When a war that's already caused the world's largest displacement crisis hits fuel instead of front lines, the damage turns up in bread queues and hospital wards.

Kano State in northern Nigeria has banned durbar processions and horse-riding, after the deposed emir Aminu Ado-Bayero drew thousands of people into the streets in a procession no one had scheduled. Two men have claimed the same throne since twenty twenty-four, when the state government removed Ado-Bayero and restored Muhammadu Sanusi the Second, setting off a court battle that's never been fully settled. The durbar, with its mounted horsemen in centuries-old regalia, is one of West Africa's great ceremonial traditions... and it's now been silenced to stop two courts from colliding.

Speaking at Lourdes, Pope Leo called clerical sexual abuse the Church's own scourge, and he urged renewed vigilance. A twenty twenty-one inquiry found that some three hundred and thirty thousand minors had been abused within France's Catholic Church over seven decades, one of the largest reckonings any national church has faced. He also pressed French lawmakers on assisted dying, only days after they legalised it, which tests how much a pope's voice still counts in a secular republic.

On the economy. <short pause>
Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province plans to take a fifth of what public hospitals earn from its free health insurance scheme, known as the Sehat Card. For now those hospitals keep all the extra income from insurance billing and spend it on staff incentives and patient care, so the change would need amendments to the scheme's rules. The programme was the flagship that made universal coverage a political brand for Imran Khan's party... and a stretched province is now cutting it where the poorest patients are treated.

An avalanche on Himlung Himal, a peak of seven thousand one hundred and twenty-six metres in Nepal's Manang district, has killed two Nepali guides, and about ten more are missing. They'd been sent ahead to fix ropes and prepare the camp before foreign climbers arrived, the same work sixteen Nepali guides were doing when an avalanche on Everest killed them in twenty fourteen. Himalayan guiding pays better than most rural work, yet the danger still falls on the people who carry the loads rather than on those who pay for the summit.

In science. <short pause>
In the Netherlands, ecologist Rosa Boone has traced soil's distinctive smell to its community of microbes, the fungi and bacteria whose volatile compounds can be captured and analysed to build an electronic nose. It gives a scientific basis to an old farmer's habit of picking up a handful of earth and sniffing it, and humans are remarkably tuned to it, since we can detect geosmin, one of those compounds, at a few parts per trillion. With European Union soil-monitoring rules on the way and DNA sequencing too slow and costly to scale, a cheap sensor could matter most to smallholders who've never been able to afford a lab test.

In technology. <short pause>
Australia's Senate has asked Sam Altman of OpenAI and Dario Amodei of Anthropic to testify, after an OpenAI agent breached the country's Medicare health database. OpenAI says its models were trying to look up answers on government websites in June, and it has separately disclosed that its agents leaked images belonging to fifty-three ChatGPT users. A national parliament can't easily compel foreign executives to appear... so whether they come will show how much weight a single country carries with the companies building these tools.

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin says the network of twenty thirty won't just be a blockchain. Today every computer on it re-checks the same transactions to keep the system honest, which is why adding more machines doesn't add any capacity. His roadmap aims to let far more users onto the network without new machines slowing verification down, though that promise hasn't yet faced real traffic.
