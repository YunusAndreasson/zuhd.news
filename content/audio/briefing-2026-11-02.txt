In politics. <short pause>
French prosecutors have opened an attempted murder investigation after petrol bombs were thrown at fifteen gendarmes, with one firebomb bursting at an officer's feet. More than seven hundred and seventy people were arrested across the country at the weekend, in a cost of living revolt that recalls the yellow vest protests of twenty eighteen, which began over a fuel tax. Whether a charge this heavy quiets the streets or hardens them is the question now facing the French government.

Bulgaria's Iliana Iotova leads the presidential run-off with about fifty-one percent, according to exit polls, while her rival Gyurov sits near forty-four, on a turnout below thirty percent. She opposes military aid to Ukraine, a striking position for a NATO member of more than two decades, and one that matters on a Black Sea coast where twenty-one people remain missing after drone attacks. These are still exit polls rather than a declared result, and a low turnout leaves open how strong any mandate would be.

In Michigan, by one former aide's account, well over half of Democratic congresswoman Haley Stevens's staff quit on Sunday after she endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over her own party's Senate nominee, Abdul El-Sayed. Her chief of staff of eight years left too and urged votes for El-Sayed, while Drop Site News reports that filings show Rogers's spokesperson is registered as a foreign agent for Israel, under a disclosure law first passed in nineteen thirty-eight to expose Nazi propaganda. Voters decide on Tuesday, and no one has yet released a count of the resignations.

Sourabh Chandrakar, accused of masterminding India's six thousand crore rupee Mahadev betting app, came back from Oman on Sunday. He hasn't been convicted of anything, and a courtroom must now test how much of that money can actually be traced.

South Africa's Khampepe Commission, which is examining why apartheid-era crimes went unprosecuted, has a new piece of evidence: a handwritten index that dates a disputed memo to two thousand and three. It supports former prosecutor Anton Ackermann's claim that a two thousand and six memo painting him as a rogue was forged, in a saga rooted in the roughly three hundred cases the Truth and Reconciliation Commission handed to prosecutors. Lawyers for victims' families found it only after inspecting the files themselves, and the commission hasn't ruled.

On the economy. <short pause>
President Bola Tinubu is seeking twenty point five billion dollars and more than one billion euros in foreign loans for Nigeria, under a supplementary borrowing plan for twenty twenty-six and twenty twenty-seven. The money is meant for infrastructure, agriculture, health, education, water, security and jobs, and it comes two decades after Nigeria won eighteen billion dollars in debt relief from the Paris Club. Senate approval hasn't been reported, and Nigerians would carry the repayments long after this government is gone.

A roti in Pakistan has gone from eight rupees to twenty-five, and flour now sells at up to one hundred and seventy rupees a kilo, more than double its price of twenty twenty-one... a rise of over two hundred percent. Dawn traces much of the damage to Punjab, the country's breadbasket since British-built canals remade its plains, where wheat buying was handed to eleven private aggregators and nine of them never started. Only one hundred and sixty thousand of three million tonnes were bought, and the government still hasn't settled its next move.

Binance customers pulled a net three point six four billion dollars from the exchange once withdrawals reopened after its six point three billion dollar hack. The company says it replaced ninety-five percent of the stolen money with its own funds, a claim that carries particular weight since the collapse of FTX in twenty twenty-two showed how quickly an exchange's assurances can fail. Binance says volumes have stabilized, and its proof of reserves, promised for the third of November, will be the first real test.

Serbia's central bank has closed Alta Bank, just days after Aleksandar Vučić lost the election, and some customers say they haven't been able to withdraw money since Saturday. Officials say clients moved large sums abroad after the vote, while deposits are insured up to fifty thousand euros, a promise that matters in a country that remembers how hyperinflation in the nineteen nineties erased family savings. What the closure says about money and power at the end of an era is a question for prosecutors and for Serbia's next government.

One family in Las Piñas, in the Philippines, paid seven hundred thousand pesos for rooftop solar and now saves twenty thousand every month. Electricity already takes around twelve percent of the median household's monthly income, and Meralco's rate has climbed sixteen percent since January, more than two decades after a landmark law restructured the country's power sector around private competition. That upfront cost is the catch, because the households most squeezed by their bills are the ones least able to pay for the way out.

In science. <short pause>
Only ten percent of desert heath myrtle survived the hot drought that struck Australia's Uluṟu-Kata Tjuṯa National Park from twenty eighteen to twenty twenty-one, the worst there on record. Surveys found that seventy-eight percent of desert oak came through it, and new research suggests that differences in water use may explain why some species die faster than others. The Aṉangu, whose ownership of this land was formally returned in nineteen eighty-five, are now watching plants they've known for generations disappear, as heat waves keep getting hotter.

In technology. <short pause>
Ukraine's air alert app sent false warnings of radiation on Sunday, along with messages about chemical threats and street combat near Kyiv... and hours later, Russia's Emergencies Ministry said its own alert app had been hit too. Ukraine blames Russia, and each side's account remains its own claim, but the danger is familiar from Hawaii in twenty eighteen, when a false missile alert sent people running for shelter. These apps only save lives if people believe them, and it's not clear how much trust will survive the next real warning.

China's SenseTime has released an open-source model that runs twenty-four layers twice, deepening its computation while staying near nine billion parameters. Sharing weights this way echoes the recurrent networks of the nineteen eighties, and it arrives from a company that's been on Washington's trade blacklist since twenty nineteen, where doing more with less computing has obvious appeal. The model reads text and images for documents, coding and tool use, but independent testers will have to judge whether looping really matches the company's claims.
