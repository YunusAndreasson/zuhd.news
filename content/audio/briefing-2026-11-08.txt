Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two tankers are burning after striking mines in and near the Strait of Hormuz... and they warn that any ship leaving Tehran's authorised route will meet the same fate. Washington is widening its own siege, with a US aircraft firing on a cargo ship leaving an Iranian port, and Central Command extending its blockade to Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea. Two rival permit regimes now govern one waterway, and civilian crews are caught between them.

In politics. <short pause>
Saudi-led air strikes killed twenty-seven villagers in al-Shariya, most of them women and children, according to the Houthi-run health ministry in Sanaa, which puts the toll of Saudi strikes since the thirteenth of July at more than a thousand killed or wounded. A war that began with Saudi intervention in twenty fifteen had been largely held in check since a truce in twenty twenty-two, and that calm has now collapsed, with government forces on the ground reporting hundreds of attacks on Houthi positions in a single day. Damascus denies sending troops, but says its training pact with Riyadh could grow into a mutual defence agreement... which would pull post-Assad Syria into a war far from its own borders.
<long pause>
The first heavy winter rain flooded more than seventy thousand tents across Gaza's displacement camps, Gaza authorities say, in figures carried by Russian state agency TASS. The families in those tents are there because their homes were destroyed, and the winterised tents, waterproof flooring and heaters they need are in short supply because of what's allowed into the strip. Civil defence crews say they lack the equipment to pull people from collapsed shelters, and the winter has barely begun.
<long pause>
Explosive-laden drones struck a military base in Santa Rosa del Sur, in Colombia's Bolívar department, killing five soldiers and wounding thirty-three, eight of them seriously. Cheap armed drones, the weapon that reshaped battlefields in Ukraine and Sudan, have now reached the armed groups that fight over Colombia's gold-mining interior, a decade after a peace deal was meant to end the country's long war. No group has claimed the attack, and authorities haven't named a suspect.
<long pause>
Three thousand three hundred people have reached El Hierro by boat since the first of November... on an island of just eleven thousand four hundred. Patrols off Senegal, Mauritania and Morocco have pushed departures further south, to The Gambia and Guinea-Bissau, stretching what's already one of the deadliest sea crossings into Europe. The island's council has declared an emergency and asked Madrid for help with housing and a strained hospital, and the boats are still arriving.
<long pause>
A gunman killed six people in Kraków's Main Square, driving in and firing at random from his car, police say, and three more bodies, two women and a man, were later found at his home. Mass shootings are rare in Poland, and this square, which draws some fifteen million visitors a year, is the heart of the country's old royal capital. Prime Minister Donald Tusk has confirmed the deaths at the house, and police haven't yet said what drove the killer.
<long pause>
New Zealand's preliminary count gives Christopher Luxon's coalition sixty-seven seats... just one more than it needs to govern. Special votes, cast overseas or outside voters' home electorates, make up fourteen percent of the total, and they've historically leaned left, shifting seats away from the right in past counts. The final result lands on the twenty-seventh of November, and until then, a single seat holds the country's government in suspense.
<long pause>
President Trump has ordered the Justice Department to investigate Democrats, naming impeachment threats and oversight letters as the alleged crime, without offering evidence. It comes days after Democrats won both chambers of Congress, and as federal judge Ana Reyes appointed former prosecutor Jessie Liu to investigate the government's own lawyers over assurances they gave her court. The department's independence from the White House was rebuilt after Watergate, and a lame-duck president is now testing whether it holds.
<long pause>
South Africa's African National Congress has fallen to about twenty percent in local elections, and the Democratic Alliance now leads Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni. It's a steep fall for the party of Nelson Mandela, which won close to seventy percent of the national vote in two thousand and four and lost its parliamentary majority twenty years later. Councils must meet within fourteen days to elect mayors, and in these fractured metros, coalition deals will decide who runs the country's economic heartland.

On the economy. <short pause>
Pakistan's already living with gas rationing and nighttime power cuts, and now an attack on a Pakistan-bound gas tanker has thrown its safe-passage understanding with Iran into doubt. Most of its liquefied natural gas comes from Qatar through Hormuz, so a separate deal with Tehran was the country's best hedge, and the same dependence left Pakistan facing blackouts during the price shock of twenty twenty-two. Islamabad must now decide whether to keep trusting that deal, or start paying more for gas from somewhere else.
<long pause>
The collapse of Ueno, a bank whose failure froze three million accounts, has ended a presidency in Paraguay. Ueno grew on regulatory favours and public-sector deposits while Santiago Peña co-owned its holding group, a stake he kept until March twenty twenty-five. The Senate impeached him on Friday despite his offer to resign, the first such removal since Fernando Lugo's one-day trial in twenty twelve, and Vice President Pedro Alliana is left to inherit the crisis.
<long pause>
China's baijiu exports to South Korea jumped eighty-five percent in nine months, to thirty-three point three million dollars, even as total baijiu exports fell by nearly half in the first half of the year. Korean convenience stores sell the spirit mixed with tonic or soda in five percent cans, diluting the pungent aroma that's long kept foreign drinkers away. With demand weak at home, China's distillers are finding that selling abroad may mean selling a different drink.
<long pause>
Africa's fifty-four countries arrive at the climate talks in Antalya demanding that last year's pledge to triple adaptation finance finally be put into practice. Article nine point one of the Paris Agreement obliges developed countries to provide that money, and they missed their earlier hundred-billion-dollar goal for years before reaching it. Now, says negotiators' chair Nana Antwi-Boasiako, rich nations are citing geopolitical tension and strain at home to hold it back again.

In science. <short pause>
A city square near the equator holds forty-two percent fewer bird species... while one at sixty-five degrees latitude loses only twelve percent. Tropical bird communities are full of habitat specialists that cities shut out, while generalists like crows thrive almost anywhere people build. Most of the world's urban growth through twenty fifty is expected in tropical Africa and Asia, exactly where the losses run steepest.
<long pause>
Physicists at Oxford used just two trapped ions to reproduce the Aharonov-Bohm effect, simulating a gauge theory on a quantum computer. First predicted in nineteen fifty-nine, the effect shows a charged particle can feel an electromagnetic potential even where no field touches it, and gauge theories of this kind underpin the Standard Model of particle physics. Classical computers struggle with these theories in higher dimensions, so the open question is how far this tiny loop can scale.

In technology. <short pause>
Binance is closing accounts across the European Union and will charge zero point zero two five percent a month on leftover balances, its emails to users say. Any account holding less than the minimum fee of ten dollars in the USD Coin stablecoin forfeits everything. Europe's Markets in Crypto-Assets rules require a licence in at least one member state, and since Binance withdrew its Greek application in June, small holders are the ones paying for that gap.
