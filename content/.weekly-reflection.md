## Top Stories This Week

**1. Iran-US War — diplomacy, blockade, and economic cascade**
The dominant story of the week across all five cycles. The arc moved from stalemate (Sunday's "progress" claims while uranium handover remains rejected) to concrete escalation (Monday's cargo ship seizure, Iran's refusal to cede Hormuz control). Pakistan's broker role deepened — Islamabad is now the only venue both sides will enter. The economic consequences spread: US Energy Secretary broke with Treasury's "summer relief" pledge, India summoned Iran's ambassador after gunboats fired on its tankers, Bangladesh gig drivers lost half their income to fuel queues. The IMF spring meetings surfaced the physical damage estimate: $58bn at Ras Laffan alone, four years to repair. The week ended with Iran saying talks are over while Pakistan insists they continue — a gap that will define next week.

**2. Israel / Palestine / Lebanon — normative collapse**
Spain gave the EU 48 hours to suspend the Association Agreement with Israel — the sharpest European diplomatic threat since the war began. Simultaneously, Turkiye's FM Fidan publicly named Israel's pattern as territorial expansion dressed as security at the Antalya Diplomacy Forum. Israel's Defence Minister Katz authorised "full force" against Lebanon under the existing ceasefire, and the week also produced the Somaliland envoy story: Israel cultivating Horn-of-Africa recognition to outflank OIC consensus. The week read as a coherent arc: the normative framework around Israel's conduct is fracturing at multiple nodes simultaneously.

**3. European elections — bloc realignment**
Hungary's Péter Magyar won a landslide over Orbán, removing the EU's most reliable unanimity veto on climate, Russia sanctions, and energy decoupling. In the same week Bulgaria held a snap election won by an anti-corruption progressive coalition. Two EU member-state elections in seven days, both moving away from the Orbán-Borisov establishment bloc. The downstream implications for Paks II (Rosatom), the 20th Russia sanctions package, and the EU-Israel Association Agreement (where Germany-Hungary blocking is cited) are direct.

**4. Global food and energy security**
The Iran-US war's second-order effects dominated this category. Daily Maverick traced converging shocks to record food import bills for African and Asian states. Pakistan negotiated emergency Qatari LNG with Hormuz still contested. India fast-tracked the Trincomalee energy hub as a Hormuz bypass. Nigeria's 2026 fiscal package pivoted to import substitution. Salvini in Italy called for a return to Russian gas. The week produced no single food-crisis story but a dense mesh of interdependencies pointing to structural fragility across the Global South.

**5. Russia's digital sovereignty push**
The Kremlin ran three moves simultaneously: VPN crackdowns, Telegram throttling, and a new national AI announcement. The pattern — claim AI modernity while closing information pluralism — is the Chinese-model playbook imported wholesale. Two cycles covered this, confirming it's a sustained campaign rather than a news peg.

## Recommendations for Next Week

1. **Title-echo: convert the style note into an executable check.** Replace the current instruction with: "List every root word in the headline. If any appear in the first sentence of the article body, flag it and rewrite the hook." This turns a vague directive into a test the editor can run mechanically rather than judge impressionistically.

2. **Investigate the acronym scanner for ISO country codes.** "IR" and "PK" dominate new violations and are two-letter strings added by the country-tag enrichment, not acronyms the writer chose. Run the scanner against a pre-country-tag version of two articles to confirm. If it is an artifact, exclude two-letter ISO codes from the acronym pattern.

3. **Force multi-source at Stage 1, not Stage 2.** Multi-source rate has been flat at ~21% for two consecutive weeks despite repeated writer instructions. The fix belongs in the selector prompt: flag any story with only one feed source as "single-source — corroboration required" and route it to Stage 1.5 prefetch explicitly targeting a second outlet. The writer cannot source what the prefetcher didn't fetch.

4. **Add Houthi / Red Sea and Russia-Ukraine as named selector requirements.** With the Iran-US arc consuming attention, both adjacent theatres went dark this week despite being direct consequences of the same conflict. Add them as explicit named coverage obligations in the selector's Middle East and Europe blocs — minimum one story per cycle when active — so they don't get squeezed by the most prominent arc.
