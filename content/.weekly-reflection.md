**1. The Iran-Hormuz war and its cascading effects** — The dominant story of the week by volume and importance. The cycle covered Iran mine-laying operations in the Strait, the U.S. deploying a third carrier strike group (first time three carriers in CENTCOM in over two decades), Iranian gunboat attacks on container ships, the seizure of the tanker Epaminondas, and Trump's reported kill-order against Iranian small boats. **2. Iran's diplomatic realignment after the ceasefire** — Araghchi's circuit — Oman, Turkey, Pakistan, and then Moscow — was the week's most analytically rich story. It maps which states Iran is now treating as its post-war perimeter: the Sunni-majority neighbours that stayed neutral (Ankara, Islamabad, Muscat) rather than the Arab Gulf capitals that either facilitated the strikes or stayed silent. The Russia leg, confirmed late Sunday, is the new development; prior cycles covered the Oman and Turkey stops. These stories belong together and should continue to be tracked as a single arc.

**3. U.S. domestic political violence and civil rights** — The WHCD shooting (three articles, merged) was the week's most-covered US domestic breaking story. Separately, the DOJ's denaturalization campaign — 287 cases in 90 days, 9× the historical average, with at least 41 targeting Palestinian-solidarity speech — is a sustained accountability story with direct relevance to Muslim readers. Both stories carried significant reader weight and were covered well.

**4. **5. Hungary's political earthquake** — Orbán's Fidesz lost a two-thirds supermajority to Tisza (Péter Magyar) — the EU's leading illiberal governance model has fallen to a defector from within it. This got one article despite being structurally significant; importance recalibrated up to 5. Worth following as Tisza forms a government and announces its EU and judicial posture.

**Press-era antipatterns** — 6 hits, up from 4. Small absolute number but moving the wrong direction. Likely clustering in Iran-war articles, which attract phrases like "the crisis" and "tensions mounted". A targeted check on those articles would confirm.

**country:null fix** — Dropped from 21 to 1. Dramatic improvement from a source-frontmatter change. This is a confirmed fix — but the ISO country code false-positive in the acronym scanner is the side-effect to resolve.

**Multi-source rate** — Stuck at 22–23% for three consecutive weeks, well below the 40% target. A structural explanation: auto-backfill articles are single-source by design and represent a material share of total output. The true editorial multi-source rate (excluding auto-backfills) is likely higher. The metric should be verified against editorial-only articles before concluding the write-prompt rule is failing.
