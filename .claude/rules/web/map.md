# The situational map

Every paragraph records a bug the map has actually had, most of which failed
silently — a wrong terminator, an unfetched basemap tier, a card that opened
and threw its story away. Read the ones touching what you are about to change.

Prayer lines and the Hijri calendar are `prayer.md` and `hijri.md` beside this;
the sun, the moon, the stars and the limb glow are `sky.md`.

## Situational map (homepage)

- Island: `public/islands/situation-map.ts` + helpers in `public/islands/_map/` (`style`, `feed`, `timeline`, `sheet`, `popup`, `solar`, `types`) and the shared chart in `public/islands/_chart.ts`. Imperative and framework-free — it stays off the Preact runtime the sheet islands use.
- **MapLibre GL renders the basemap**, from GeoJSON and SDF glyphs served from our own origin (`scripts/build/basemap.js` → `/basemap/`). No tile provider, no API key, no third-party request: the CSP stays `default-src 'none'` apart from the blob: worker MapLibre spawns. **The basemap is Natural Earth 1:50m, fetched once** (~1.6 MB, 547 KB gzipped), with 1:10m still behind `ULTRA_ZOOM` 5.5 for close work. **The swap must fetch the file and hand `setData` a parsed object**: MapLibre 6 dropped the URL form of `GeoJSONSource.setData`, and passing a string throws nothing, fires no `error` and makes no request — the upper tier was built, deployed and never once fetched.
- **The world is a sphere at the view every reader opens on** (2026-08-01), interpolated to Mercator between `GLOBE_ZOOM.sphere` (3) and `.plane` (5) by a `projection.type` expression in `_map/style.ts`. Not decoration: **Mercator at world zoom is a lie about area**, drawing Greenland the size of Africa, and this file spends four hundred lines making sure a *tone* cannot overstate a value while the projection underneath overstated every high-latitude country. It is the same objection that turned `renderWorldCopies` off — a map that misplaces or misproportions the world is lying about where things are — and the fix is the projection rather than a caption. **Do not replace it with `projection: { type: 'globe' }`**: that is a preset expanding to an 11 → 12 interpolation (`projection_factory.ts`), and this map's `maxZoom` is 9, so `'globe'` is a sphere at every reachable zoom and the flat half of the design silently never runs. The costs are real and stated: a sphere hides a hemisphere, so half the stories are *absent* rather than dimmed until the reader turns it, which is why `Popup` carries `locationOccludedOpacity: 0` — a card anchored to Santiago while the camera is on the Levant would otherwise render in front of the Earth, pointing at a beacon that is not there.
- **The graticule is what makes the sphere read as one** (2026-08-01). The projection was making a claim the drawing could not support. Space and sea are the *same colour by construction* — `--map-ground` is `MAP_COLOURS.ocean`, which is what lets every scrim over the canvas keep meaning what it meant on the flat map — and `draw_sky.ts` lights the atmosphere from the sun's own direction, so the limb is a crescent along the day side and **unlit everywhere else**. Half the planet had no edge at all: it ended wherever the last coastline happened to be. Darkening space to give it one is not available, and the arithmetic is the same wall the terminator hit over water: the ocean is `#080a0d`, so *everything* below it is inside 1.09:1 of black. A graticule answers it from the other direction, and it is the honest answer rather than a clever one — **the curvature of the lines is the encoding**. Meridians converging toward a pole and parallels bowing away from the equator are what a sphere looks like at any tone on any ground, so the cue costs no contrast, works identically on the day and night sides, and its outermost arcs trace exactly the limb the atmosphere cannot draw. `_map/graticule.ts`, 30° spacing (12 meridians, 5 parallels), walked at 5° so a meridian stays smooth where the globe compresses longitude hardest. Meridians stop at **85°, not the pole**: twelve lines meeting at a point draw a star at each cap — a loud mark at the two places this map has least to say about — and within a few degrees of the pole they are inside one pixel of each other and composite into a dot. Generated, not fetched: 761 points of arithmetic would otherwise be a request and an entry in `BASEMAP_V`. Nothing crosses the antimeridian (a parallel runs −180 to +180 and stops), so the bar-across-the-map failure `prayer.ts` records cannot arise; the −180 meridian is drawn and its +180 twin is not, because with `renderWorldCopies: false` they are the same edge of the same world.
- **It is drawn *under* `land`, and that is what makes it tunable at all.** There is no tone that works flat for a grid line — the argument `MAP_COLOURS.water` already makes about rivers. A line quiet enough on near-black sea is darker than every stop of `LAND_RAMP` and vanishes over the continents; anything that survives the ramp's bright end is loud over water, where the beacons are. Measured: at `#232833` above the land it was legible over the Indian Ocean and simply absent across Africa. Inserted before `land` — the escape `day-shade` takes — the question does not arise: one ground, tuned against it, **1.44:1 on the ocean** against a frontier's 3.3, a prayer hairline's ~1.5 and a week-old beacon's 1.89. The second reason is the better one: sent under the land the grid **crosses not one country**, so the map gains the cue and loses no legibility anywhere the data actually is. It fades out on `GLOBE_ZOOM.sphere` → `.plane` read from the constants, so the grid is gone at exactly the zoom the sphere it explains stops existing; on a flat map at street scale a graticule is a grid over a city.
- **The 1.44:1 was the tone's, and for a month nothing on screen ever reached it** (2026-08-02). Two separate slips, both of the same shape — *a figure taken about a colour and quoted about a mark.* First, `line-width` was **0.6**, and MapLibre's line shader extrudes to `outset = halfwidth + 1/DPR/2`, so a 0.3 half-width sits inside its own antialiasing falloff and the stroke never reaches full alpha along any part of its length. Second, the paragraph above used to say `night-shade` left the grid "effectively undimmed at night, because 0.28 black over near-black water is about two values in 255" — which is an argument about the **ground**, not about the line drawn on it. A multiply takes 28% of whatever it lands on: three values off the ocean, eleven off the line. The absolute step is negligible and the *ratio* is not. Measured off the built map over open water, the grid came back at **1.00–1.06:1** — not a faint cue, absent; a 16× exposure boost renders it perfectly and nothing else does. `line-width` is **1.2** now, measured after at **1.24:1 on the night ocean against 1.10:1 before**. And the retina assumption was wrong too: `ANTIALIASING` is 0.25 at DPR 2 so the arithmetic says 0.3 should clear it, but a `shoot.mjs` capture at DPR 2 gives `rgb(28,32,40)` at 1.2 against `rgb(18,21,26)` at 0.6 — **47% of the tone, not 100%**. A half-width under one *device* pixel loses coverage whatever the falloff term says. It stays under `land` and therefore keeps paying `night-shade`'s 28%: above it means above the land, where the paragraph above says no tone works, and hiding it again would take a second `land` fill duplicating the ramp expression with nothing checking the two agree.
- **`globeFitZoom` is not `worldFitZoom` with a different name, and shipping one without the other would have opened the map on a marble.** MapLibre sizes the sphere as `radius = worldSize / 2π / cos(lat)`, so the globe's **diameter** is `TILE_PX · 2^z / (π · cos lat)` against Mercator's `TILE_PX · 2^z` of *width*. At the flat fit zoom the sphere is about **a third of the canvas width** — 379px of 1104. Nothing would have reported it, because both numbers are honestly describable as "the fit zoom". It takes the **shorter** side where the flat fit took the longer one: `renderWorldCopies: false` made MapLibre refuse any zoom at which the flat world failed to *cover* the canvas, since a world narrower than its frame is duplicated rather than letterboxed — a sphere cannot duplicate, it is a disc, and it is *meant* to be letterboxed by space. Capped at `GLOBE_ZOOM.sphere` so a 4K monitor (which fits the globe at ~3.4) cannot open part-way through the flattening, on a view no reader chose and neither projection was tuned for. `GLOBE_FIT` is an honest fudge factor: the formula gives the *world*-space radius, perspective foreshortens it, and measured across three viewports the rendered diameter comes out ~0.87 of what the formula asks on a desktop and ~0.93 on a phone.
- **The effective zoom floor sits ~0.109 below the number handed to `minZoom`, and that is not a bug.** On the sphere MapLibre clamps to `minZoom + getZoomAdjustment(0, lat)`, which is `minZoom + log2(cos lat)` — it re-applies the latitude term itself, to hold the planet's apparent size constant as the reader moves north or south. At the home latitude (21.4°, Makkah) that is −0.105, so the reader can pull out about 11% past a perfect fit. Harmless here, because on this projection the space around the disc *is* the design; written down because a floor that does not equal the number given to it looks like a defect to anyone who measures it.
- **Space is transparent, so the chrome seam is a CSS fix.** On the sphere MapLibre draws the `ocean` background layer *on the tile meshes* — on the planet — and clears the canvas to `Color.transparent` with the context created `alpha: true`. Outside the limb there is no MapLibre colour at all, so `.map-canvas-host` paints `--map-ground` behind it. Space then equals `MAP_COLOURS.ocean` **by construction**, and every gradient in `.map-hud` and `.map-timeline` that fades into `--map-ground` keeps meaning what it meant on the flat map; `colour-system.test.js` already pins the two sides of that seam. Any other tone for space would put a second ground under those scrims with no way for them to tell which one they are over. The consequence is deliberate: with space and sea identical, the planet's edge is drawn by the atmosphere alone.
- **The atmosphere is lit from wherever `style.light` says, and for a month this map never said** (2026-08-02). `drawAtmosphere` gets `u_sun_pos` from `getSunPos(light, transform)`, which applies the camera rotation **only when `light.anchor === 'map'`**. The style declared `sky` and never declared `light`, so the anchor defaulted to `'viewport'`, `lightMat` stayed the identity, and the uniform was a constant vector in view space — the default `position: [1.15, 210, 30]`, which works out to up-and-to-the-left. The globe wore a lit crescent **nailed to the upper-left corner of the screen**, the same at every hour of every day from every camera: five captures between 01:44 and 01:56 Makkah, the whole visible hemisphere in night, and it never moved. It disagreed with the terminator drawn underneath it and with `starfield.ts`'s crescent, which was computed from the real sun the whole time — two atmospheres over one planet, one of them pointing the wrong way, and every note under this bullet written as though the sun were driving it. `drawSolar` now sets it from `sunLightPosition` on the existing 120-second tick. Two things there are worth keeping: MapLibre **negates** `position` before the shader sees it, so what is handed over is the *antisolar* direction (established from the default's own observable crescent, not from the docs); and `anchor: 'map'` means the value is in the world frame and MapLibre re-rotates it per frame, so a pan cannot stale it and the light needs no camera argument. **None of the other six `sky` properties reach the globe.** `sky-color` and `horizon-color` feed the flat-map sky program, which needs pitch this map does not have; `fog-*` is set only in `terrainUniformValues` and that path hard-zeroes it with `isGlobeMode ? 0 : …`. There is no atmospheric perspective to be had from MapLibre on a sphere, and no setting of any of them will light the night limb — the shader is a Rayleigh/Mie integral and returns zero where the sun is behind the planet. That is what the airglow in `sky.md` is for.
- **`atmosphere-blend` is 0.34, not the 0.8 default, and the number was measured.** It is a *crescent* along the day limb rather than a ring — see the bullet above for where "the day limb" is actually taken from. At 0.8 that crescent renders near-white — on a map where `OVERLAY_COLOUR.genocide` is the one unmuted tone the whole palette is rationed to afford, a thousand-pixel white arc drawn by the *camera* was louder than the gravest mark on the display. At 0.34 it measures **1.49:1 against the ocean**, the same register as the prayer hairlines, which is the right one for furniture. The night limb stays unlit and therefore invisible against space; that is kept, because it is what the terminator already says, said once more by the edge — what made it read as *clipping* was the day side shouting. `atmosphere-blend` takes a constant and must not be zoom-interpolated: `draw_sky.ts` multiplies it by `projectionTransition`, so it already fades out in step with the globe, and an interpolation here would be a second copy of `GLOBE_ZOOM` that nothing checks against the first.
- **The projection re-keyed every zoom gate on this map, and nothing said so** (`DETAIL_ZOOM`). A globe is drawn far smaller than a flat world at the same zoom, so fitting one to the canvas lands about a **whole zoom level higher**: measured, the opening view went **1.25 → 2.36 at 1600px, 1.58 → 2.62 at 1920, 2.04 → 3.00 at 2560**. Every `minzoom` and every `['step', ['zoom'], …]` in `_map/style.ts` was chosen by looking at the flat map, so all of them now sit a level lower *relative to the view* than they were written for. Two were crossed outright — `place-dots`/`place-labels` at 2.4 and `rivers` at 1.6 — so the opening frame acquired every city name and the whole river network on the day it became a globe, which is most of why it read as busy. `globeFitZoom` caps at `GLOBE_ZOOM.sphere`, so **3 is the highest zoom the map can open at on any screen**, which makes it the one honest floor for close-up detail; `DETAIL_ZOOM` is that cap plus a margin, and the three layers now share it. **A new `minzoom` below it is a layer that will appear at world view.**
- **The density wash was checked and deliberately not retuned.** It looked like the next thing to compress, and the arithmetic says otherwise: the globe's scale at the centre is *higher* than the flat map's (5.78 px/degree against 4.27), so the wash covers **9.7° of longitude where the flat map's covered 10.9°**, and a 7.5px beacon covers *less* geography than it did, not more. Nothing in `DENSITY_STOPS`, `GAUSS_COEF` or `placeDensity` needed to move. What the wash does still require is that it finish fading before the plane arrives — its kernels are the only ones sized in screen pixels, so they compress toward the limb — and `DENSITY_FADE_OUT` (5) equals `GLOBE_ZOOM.plane` exactly. `map-island.test.js` reads both off the style handed to the engine and fails if they part.
- **Country hover is `feature-state`, and was a `setFilter` on every pointer move for as long as the layer existed.** A filter change ends in `Style._updateLayer`, which marks the layer's whole source `'reload'` — so moving the pointer across Africa re-bucketed `countries`, 1.6 MB and 99k points, once per country. The rule was already written down one layer up for the beacons ("hover is `promoteId` + `setFeatureState`, not a `setPaintProperty` rewrite per pointer move") and had simply never been applied here; nothing could notice, because a filter rewrite is *correct*, only expensive. **Never clear this source wholesale to unset it**: `removeFeatureState({source})` with no id is what `applyMetric` uses, and on `countries` that map holds every country's `p` — the entire ground metric. The previous id is unset by key, and `countryHoverWritten` tracks what is *written* rather than where the pointer is, because `applyMetric` wipes the hover bit along with the metric.
- **`global-state` was evaluated for the scrub path and rejected on measurement** (2026-08-01). It is the obvious modern answer — one `setGlobalStateProperty` in place of three `setFilter` calls and a 720-feature `setData` per scrub tick — and it does not do what it appears to. `Style._applyGlobalStateChanges` splits by where the reference lives: a **paint** property gets `_updatePaintProperty` only, but a `filter` is folded into `getLayoutAffectingGlobalStateRefs` and reloads the source, and `visibility` ends in `recalculateVisibility() + _updateLayer` — which is exactly what `setLayoutProperty` already does. The trap is the paint case: `StyleLayer.setPaintProperty` returns `isDataDriven()` as its `requiresRelayout` flag, so any expression that reads `['get', …]` *and* `global-state` — which the story decay curve and the country-hover tint both must — reloads the source just like the filter it replaced. **`global-state` is cheap only for data-constant paint, and this map has no such property worth driving.** The eight `setLayoutProperty` visibility toggles and the three `applyTimeFilters` `setFilter`s are already the right tool; the latter must stay filters, because `queryRenderedFeatures` returns *placed* symbols regardless of opacity, so hiding a mark by paint would leave it hoverable with nothing on screen.
- **`rivers` draws on `line-layer-opacity`** (new in MapLibre 6.0), and the reason is a claim the layer must not make. Natural Earth ships a river system as separate features, so a tributary and the stem it joins are two strokes laid over each other for the length of the junction, and under `line-opacity` those alphas accumulate. Measured on 6.1 with two crossing lines at 0.5: each arm renders rgb(128), the crossing rgb(192) — `1 − (1 − 0.5)²`, textbook compositing. So every confluence drew brighter than the rivers feeding it, which reads as *more river*, and the note on `line-width` says why that is wrong: a river is not a quantity, and varying weight would imply one. (6.0's changelog does mention fixing overlapping-line artefacts for `line-opacity`; measured, that fix does not cover this case.) The cost is one extra full-canvas pass per *rendered* frame — `draw_layer_opacity.ts` renders the layer to a scratch framebuffer and composites once — not per idle tick, since a still map draws no frames. `prayer-lines` is deliberately left on `line-opacity`: `line-layer-opacity` is `data-constant` in the spec and cannot carry that layer's `feature-state` hover step, and splitting one decision across both properties would express it as a product of two numbers.
- **Three MapLibre 6 features were audited and deliberately not adopted.** `reduceMotion` on the map options is an **override**, not an opt-in: `browser.prefersReducedMotion` already reads `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')` and `handler_manager.ts` already gates gesture inertia on it, so passing the option would *replace* the reader's preference with a constant. `text-overlap: 'cooperative'` keeps **both** labels when two collide with each other, which is precisely the failure the market numeral's placement rules exist to prevent ("31.6%" out of two true numbers) — the droppable labels want `never`, which is what `text-allow-overlap: false` already gives them. And **a bare `map.on('error')` would make things worse**: `Evented.fire` prints an `ErrorEvent` to `console.error` *when it has no listeners*, and `scripts/perf/probe.mjs` captures `console.error` and sets a non-zero exit — so `npm run perf` already fails on a MapLibre error, and adding a handler would swallow the one thing that makes it fail.
- **`prewarm()` runs at the top of `mount`, and `clearPrewarmedResources()` pairs with `map.remove()`.** The whole HUD, rail, scrubber and style are built before `new MapLibreMap`, so the worker pool is created during work we are doing anyway rather than on demand. It is explicitly *not* aimed at this map's actual boot cost: ~820ms of ~1.9s is `getShaderParameter`/`getProgramParameter` blocking the main thread on the driver, still without `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` in 6.1.0, and untouchable from here.
- **The `as never` casts are gone, and twelve of the fourteen were never needed.** They sat on the eight layers carrying the map's most complex expressions — the ones the checker was least able to watch — and `never` disables checking on the *whole* object rather than the offending property. Removing them all left exactly two errors, both from `_map/markets.ts`: a style expression is a *tuple* to MapLibre's types, and TypeScript only narrows an array literal to one when it has an expected type to narrow against. Written inline in an `addLayer` call it does; returned from a bare `() => ({…})` it does not, and the literals widen to `(string | number | string[])[]`. Naming the return types (`NonNullable<SymbolLayerSpecification['layout' | 'paint']>`) and typing the shared `TONE` expression restores contextual typing at the literal, which is where it belongs.
- **The bottom strip is one row of four summaries, and every member is behind a fold-up panel** (2026-08-01). It was two rows — the exchange tally with its named movers, then eleven currency, metal and crypto quotes — costing 97 to 141px of map depending on width, and everything that followed was width management: culling the two comparison currencies below 1300px, halving the movers at the same breakpoint, dropping the movers entirely on a phone, and a `flex: 1 1 34rem` basis swept against real line counts to stop the currencies group breaking mid-group. All of it was the same problem, and the phone block had already named the answer in as many words: the tally is what the strip exists for ("how is the world moving", which a map cannot answer by being looked at) and the members are the follow-up. Four summaries now, each `[net tick] label ⌃n ⌄n`, and **the strip is 22px**. The net tick is not derived from the counts — a group can be 4 up and 3 down and still net down, and those are two different facts.
- **The panel is a non-modal `<dialog>`, and both halves of that matter.** `show()` rather than `showModal()`, because a backdrop that makes the page inert and takes focus is more ceremony than a glance at a ticker deserves — the map stays live underneath and a second press of the same summary closes it. Still a `<dialog>` rather than a positioned `<div>`, because `.map-markets` sits inside the scrubber's clipped, scrimmed box and an in-flow panel is cut off by it; the top layer ignores ancestor clipping for non-modal dialogs exactly as it does for modal ones. The cost is that the platform stops doing three things: Escape, the light dismiss and focus are all wired by hand. The Escape listener is registered at strip construction, which is *before* the island's own `keydown`, so it runs first and `stopPropagation` keeps the same key from also resetting the camera — the island's stated "innermost first" order, honoured by registration rather than by asking. The light dismiss is `pointerdown`, not `click`: a `click` listener on `document` fires *after* the summary's own handler on the way back up, so the first version opened the panel and immediately closed it again.
- **The surplus width is free, and that is the whole shape of the desktop layout** (2026-08-01). A disc in a 16:9 frame leaves the sides empty, and measured against the built map the canvas ran **39% empty at 1600×900, 41% at 1920, 44% at 2560 and 60% at 3440**. The first reading of that was "none of it can be given back to the planet", which is true and was taken for the end of the argument; the corollary went unwritten for a week. The globe is `min(canvasW, canvasH)` and the canvas is as tall as the window, so **width taken away from the canvas costs the Earth nothing at all until the canvas is square** — at 1920 that is 577px the planet is not using and cannot use, free in the strict sense that removing them changes not one rendered pixel of it. That is what the instrument rail is made of.
- **There are two layouts, and there used to be three** (2026-08-03). `body.map-wide` was gated on *measured surplus width* — `viewportW − railW − viewportH ≥ 18rem`, computed by `syncWide` because the condition mixes a width, a height and a rem-valued column and the aspect ratio that satisfies it therefore moves with the viewport's height (1.99 at 600px tall, 1.74 at 800, 1.46 at 1295), which no `min-aspect-ratio` can express. The arithmetic was right and the *gate* was the mistake: every desktop that failed it fell back to a third layout — chips in a strip across the top of the map, the money block and its sparklines exiled to the scrubber's readout line and hidden behind hover — so **which side of the map the controls stood on, and whether the sparklines were on screen at all, depended on the shape of the reader's window.** That is the same complaint this file already makes about the strip's own height thrashing between 1200 and 1400px, one level up. The rail is the desktop layout at every desktop width now; `syncWide` asks one question, `matchMedia('(min-width: 901px)')`, off `NARROW_PX` so the island and the stylesheet quote one threshold. It stays a class rather than a bare media query because the whole HUD is built by the island — there is no server-rendered chrome for CSS to lay out alone — and because `placeMarketStrip` has to answer the same question to pick the readout's parent. It still runs **before `new MapLibreMap`**: `fitZoom` reads `mapEl.clientWidth` and the opening zoom is also `minZoom`, so setting the class afterwards opens every desktop at a zoom fitted to a canvas a rail too wide, with no gesture back out of it. The surplus argument survives as the argument for `--map-aside-w`'s cap, which is the next bullet.
- **The rail ladder gained a bottom step, and that is what pays for the rail at a laptop width.** With the gate gone, both columns stand at every desktop width, and below about 1400px the surplus term is negative — so `--map-aside-w` sits on its 18rem floor and the story rail is the only thing left that can give. `--map-rail-base` steps to **17rem below 1200px** (`min-width: 901px` too, since a phone has no instrument rail to make room for). At 1024×768 that is a 464px canvas against the 400px a flat 21rem would have left; 272px is still ~32 characters of headline, one rung above the 15rem the `clamp()` floors at. The cap at the other end is typographic and this is the same rule run downward: the widest a headline column may be *here* is what the map can spare.
- **The rail takes what its contents want, not the whole surplus.** The first version took the leftover entire, on the reasoning that spending all of it lands the canvas at 1:1 — the frame a disc actually wants. It produced a 512px column at 1920 for content whose widest block needs about 300, which reads as a panel nobody finished. `--map-aside-w` is `clamp(18rem, surplus, 21rem)`, so the cap is the content's and the remainder goes back to the map as margin. Canvas 1176×913 at 1920 and 1968×1295 at 2560, against 1512×935 and 2112×1295 before: materially squarer, the planet unchanged to the pixel. Ultrawide keeps its space, as it should.
- **Same DOM, different axis.** `.map-hud` *is* the rail — every rule is a `body.map-wide` override, and the phone block is untouched. The markup was already right; its problem was that a row has one dimension to group in. Thirteen chips and four separators in a wrap run can only say "these are all controls"; the same thirteen in a column with the separators drawn across it say which three groups they are, and each group gained a one-word name (`stories`, `layers`, `ground`) set one rung below an unlit chip and **without the glyph column**, so a heading starts at the rail's edge while every chip's label starts 22px in — which is what separates the two, since both are `--map-ink-dim`. It ends three width negotiations at once, each of which this file records as a bug: the clock's measured `padding-right` reservation, the money ribbon's swept `flex: 1 1 34rem`, and the ≤1300px cull of two currencies. None can arise in a column, and once the strip stopped being a desktop layout at all, four things could be deleted rather than merely overridden: **`--map-status-w`** and the measurement that published it (the clock is laid out in the rail, not painted over a row that has to reserve its width); **`anchorLegend`** and `--legend-x` (the legend is in flow in both surviving layouts — a column grows downward by definition, and on the phone the box is `display: contents`); the `@media (max-width: 1250px)` block that hid the clock on a narrow desktop, which would now hide the rail's own head; and `.map-filters { display: contents }`, whose only purpose was letting chips escape their group into the strip's wrap run.
- **The scrubber stays at the foot and stops spanning the window.** A time axis reads left to right and no amount of spare width on the right is a reason to turn it. Confined to the canvas column it is the same fortnight at a legible rhythm rather than 105px per day with a sliver in each bucket.
- **The money readout is the head of the instrument rail, and the bar it replaced lasted a day** (2026-08-02). That bar's argument was recorded here and was good: "it is not a control and not a legend, so neither rail is its home; it is a *readout about the world* and it belongs where a reader meets it before they start working the map." Every clause is true and the conclusion followed from a premise about the rail rather than about the readout — **the rail held controls, so a readout could not go in it.** This is what changes. It is the *instrument* rail; an instrument is a thing that shows a reading; and the block sits at `order: 1`, above every control in the column, so a reader still meets it first. What the column buys is the dimension the bar never had. A row has width to spend and a readout on it can carry **breadth** — a three-segment bar at true share — which is one number about today. A column can carry a **line**, which is thirty observations, and a line is the one thing about the world's money that this map could not otherwise state at any length. So `.map-marketbar` is gone with its scrim, `breadth()` and `.map-markets-breadth` with it, and the counts went where the reader's own answer put them: into the panel. Four groups (`markets`, `currencies`, `metals`, `crypto`) and a second block of three world series the payload has always carried and nothing ever read — Brent, the VIX, the US 10-year.
- **Every sparkline covers the same calendar window, and the rule it replaced counted the wrong thing** (2026-08-03). This used to read "every sparkline draws the same 30 observations, and that is a correctness rule rather than a preference", and the argument under it was right: the series behind these rows are 30 to 66 points long — the FX basket publishes 30 days, an exchange publishes a quarter of sessions, `brent` and `vix` about 60 — so drawn at their natural lengths the column is seven pictures of seven periods set to one rhythm, with the printed percentage beside each measuring a different span. That is the `reference: 'open'` trap in `charts.md` arriving one level up. **`SPARK_WINDOW` could not prevent it, because thirty observations is thirty calendar days on the FX basket and about six weeks on an exchange trading five days in seven.** The unit was the bug, and nothing could notice: every row drew a line of the right shape over the wrong period, and the constant's own docblock stated the invariant it was failing. The window is a number of **days** now, taken from the rail's one time range, and the rows genuinely do cover one period. The dates it needs are not in any payload — `periods[i]` is a pre-formatted `"Jul 27"` with the ISO date discarded at fetch time in every one of `scripts/lib/trends-sources/*.js` — so `_map/series-window.ts` reconstructs them by walking **backwards** from the series-level `asOf`, decrementing the year wherever a month steps up in reverse. Sound because nothing here spans twelve months; `null` rather than a guess when a label fails to parse, and the row falls back whole to the old count window rather than mixing the two.
- **A series that cannot fill the window is drawn short, which is the only thing that stops it lying.** The FX basket publishes 30 days and an exchange publishes a quarter, so at the 90d step three of the seven rows have a third of the period the control names. Stretched across the box they are indistinguishable from the rows that really do cover it — the same picture standing for two different facts. `coverage()` returns where the data sits inside the asked-for window as a 0–1 pair and `_spark.ts` maps the polyline into that sub-range, so those rows start two thirds of the way in and the empty left is the statement. Measured off the built page: `x0` is 67.8 of 100 for currencies, metals and crypto at 90d, and 2.2 to 7.8 for the four that fill it.
- **The 24h step is a slope, not an autoscaled diagonal.** Every series here is daily closes — there is no intraday data anywhere in `content/trends/*.json`, `.markets.json` or `.stocks-cache.json` — so one day is exactly two observations. That is a real line and a useless one autoscaled: two points fitted to their own domain are a full-height diagonal whatever they are, so a −0.02% day and a −2.9% day draw identically. It draws against a **fixed ±3% domain** instead (`SeriesOptions.domain`, added for this), so slope is proportional to the move, a calm day reads flat and a violent one steep. Fixed rather than fitted to the day's own spread for the reason the density field is not rescaled to the visible set: rows then compare with each other *and* across days, and a quiet day is allowed to look quiet. 3 is measured — the FX basket rarely clears 0.5% in a day, the metals and the exchanges about 1%, crypto 2–3%. Moves past it clamp, and **the figure beside the line is never clamped**: the row still prints what happened.
- **A group's composite is only as long as its shortest member, and four exchanges were deciding it for thirty.** `meanIndex` aligns by index and takes the last N where N is the shortest, which is right for series of comparable length and catastrophic with one gappy member. Measured against a real payload: Tadawul, DFM, SET and PSE publish series with multi-week holes, so inside a 7-day window they hold **one** observation against a median of six — and the world's thirty equity indices were drawing as a two-point straight line, a clean confident trend sourced from the member least able to support it. It moved the *number* too, not only the shape (7d read −0.2% against the +1.0% the set actually did). A member now joins if it carries at least four fifths of what the median member carries; half was tried and still let an 11-session member set N for a 21-session window. The filter is abandoned below two survivors — a rule that can empty its own input is not a rule — and what it drops it reports, so the row's label says "from 26 of 30 with a full window". A bounded set that does not state its bound reads as the whole, which is the rule `fetch-firms.js` already keeps with `skipped`.
- **The rail is grouped by subject, and every row has one grammar** (2026-08-03). Two row families had drifted into looking identical and behaving differently: `[mark] LABEL ~~~ +x%` opened a panel in the money block and toggled a layer among the chips, same shape, different verb. Three moves settle it. `prayers` loses its switch — it is a **geometry**, computed from the sun for the instant on the clock, true the way the terminator beside it is true, and the terminator has never had one; a control over a constant only ever answers yes, and what it really offered was a way to tidy the map, which is what the fold is for. `markets` and `straits` move to the money block, where a published economic series belongs — the exchange composite was *already* drawn there, so a `markets` chip carrying a trend would have been the same line twice fifteen rows apart, and a chokepoint's daily vessel transits are a fact about trade filed between `thermal` and `conflict`. What is left in `layers` is four hazards and one determination.
- **A mark means there is something to switch.** That is the grammar, and it is why a story chip has a dot and `BRENT` does not. The money rows that control a layer carry the layer's silhouette in a `.map-filter-mark` column and pressing *it* toggles; pressing the rest of the row opens the panel. The switch is a **sibling** of the summary and never inside it — a `<button>` within a `<button>` is invalid and browsers drop the inner one, which is the trap the rail head already records for its disclosure and refresh pair. Rows with nothing to switch get an **empty** mark column rather than no element: without it the labels of the rows that have one would start 22px right of the rows that do not, and a column whose left edge moves twice reads as three lists.
- **The phone keeps the two chips the desktop hides, and that is not a duplicate.** `markets` and `straits` are still built into `.map-filters` and hidden by `body.map-wide` — because the phone has no money block to carry their switch (the strip is one wrapped line of the scrubber, a readout with no room for a control), and the `layers` panel is the only place a phone reader reaches a layer at all. Dropping them would have removed function from mobile, which is the test that panel has always had to pass: nothing hidden that the reader cannot get to. The two surfaces share one state — a chip press calls `marketStrip.setLayerState` and a switch press repaints the chip — so a reader who crosses the breakpoint never finds them disagreeing.
- **Every sparkline on the rail starts on one column, and the label width is measured.** They began where each label ended, so thirteen rows started at thirteen different x; the lines all *ended* flush, which is what hid it, since a ragged left against a flush right reads as centring. `--map-row-label-w` is **4.8rem**, from `currencies` rendering 72px at `--size-xs` in all-small-caps at `--track-wide` — the widest name either family has. The first value was 6.2rem, guessed from `--map-spine-w`, which is measured at 11px for a different job and spent 27px of every line on nothing. Two things bit on the way: both families are `<button>`s and **a button centres its text**, invisible while the box was content-sized and the whole problem once it was not; and `.map-markets-group` carries `flex-wrap: wrap`, so the moment the label took a fixed width the line no longer fit beside it and **dropped to a second row underneath its own label** — both then measuring from the same left edge, which is exactly the alignment being asked for and the reason it took a measurement to see rather than a look.
- **`.map-markets-item` is `display: contents` outside the rail, and `gap: inherit` had to come with it.** The wrapper that makes a switch and a summary one row on the desktop is a block box the phone's wrap run cannot have — it put every group on its own line. Dissolving it fixed the lines and broke the spacing instead: `.map-markets-group` sets `gap: inherit`, which reads its *parent's* gap, and the parent is now the wrapper. Without passing it through, the phone's line closed up to `markets13 14 3 flat18 closed`. Same trick and the same correction as `.map-range-group`.
- **The chips carry trends too, and the tone rule inverts there** (2026-08-03). The money block had the only lines on the rail, and the two groups under it ask the same question about a different quantity — *is there more of this than there was*. A reader deciding whether to switch a layer on has no way to know it is spiking, and the chip is where that decision is made, so the line goes on the chip. **What does not travel with it is `--map-pos`/`--map-neg`.** Those mean "a signed change" and read as good and bad; on a market that is a convention old enough to be invisible, and on a hazard count it is a claim — a green **DISASTERS +40%** is this map telling a reader that more disasters is good news. Same trap the genocide caption records, where colour on a label made the gravest mark on the display read as an alert about the interface. So identity rides on the chip's own `--cat`, the hue its glyph is already drawn from; direction rides on the shape, which is what a sparkline is for; magnitude rides on the figure. None of the three is a verdict. The money keeps green and orange and is now the only place on the rail that has them, which is what makes them mean something.
- **A switched-off chip keeps its line, in the ink its label already steps down to.** That is most of the point of putting it there: the reason to turn a layer on is that something is happening in it, and a rail that hid the evidence behind the switch would be asking the reader to guess. It is the rule the folded rail already keeps — the controls go, the readings stay — applied one level down. `--map-ink-dim` is the fallback on `.map-filter-spark` rather than `currentColor`, deliberately: at `currentColor` an unlit line would follow the *label* into its ink step while a lit one followed `--cat`, which is two rules for one channel depending on state.
- **Three chips of eleven can support a line, and the other eight are blank for reasons worth stating.** **conflict** is UCDP, which publishes months in arrears — the newest event in a real payload is **125 days old**, so every step this control offers is empty, which is the same fact the layer's own decay curve already anchors on its dataset rather than on wall-clock. **thermal** is eleven detections from a single satellite pass. **famine** and **genocide** are determinations rather than events, which is why `map-island.test.js` fails if a time filter appears on them — and a trend line is a time filter with a picture. **prayers** is a geometry, not a quantity. **markets** has its line in the money block above, where it is one of seven rather than the only one, and its chip has no single `--cat` to draw in anyway. Leaving eight rows blank is the honest shape of the data; inventing a line for any of them would be the more confident and worse answer.
- **A count is a rate, so it is judged by halves, and it is bucketed across the window that is actually covered.** `halfOverHalf` compares the later half of the window against the earlier, because last-against-first on a bucketed count is one bucket against one other bucket — at the day step, "the 11pm hour had three stories and midnight had one, down 67%". Both halves use every observation on their side, which is the cheapest estimator that answers "is this rising". And the buckets are laid across what the payload **covers**, not across what the reader asked for: GDACS holds a rolling ~16 days, so bucketed across a 30-day range the fourteen days it does not hold arrived as empty buckets, landed almost entirely in the earlier half, and the chip read **+911.1%** — a real division over an absence, which is the most convincing kind of wrong number. Over the covered period it reads +63.2%, and `coverage` still draws the line short so the reader can see it does not reach. A first half with genuinely nothing in it prints no figure at all: a rise from nothing has no percentage.
- **Straits go through the money block's arithmetic rather than a second copy of it.** Unlike the stories and the disasters, the chokepoints have a *published* series — eleven of them, eighty daily vessel-transit counts each — so the chip calls `sparkInput`: date-windowed, composited by `meanIndex`, short members set aside, short-drawn when it cannot fill the range. The day step needs each member's own last-against-previous, which is `seriesChangePct`; `delta7vs90` is on the payload, is a week against a quarter, and passing it here would print a figure the line does not draw.
- **A group's line is a composite, and `dollarIndex` had already written it.** Four of the seven rows are groups, not instruments, so they have no series of their own; `meanIndex` normalises each constituent to its own first value, averages, and rebases to 100. That function *is* the old `dollarIndex` with the dollar-specific reasoning moved to its call site — the arithmetic was already there, already argued, and already shipping. Two things it must be fed correctly and neither is checked by anything: the currencies use `TickerEntry.values`, which is the **inverted** `1/x` series, or a collapsing basket draws as a rally; and `usd-index` is excluded from its own group, because it is derived from the other six and moves opposite to them by construction, so averaging it back in cancels a seventh of the signal. Normalising before averaging is what makes the mean mean anything — a raw mean over an exchange level near 3.3 million and one near 1,400 is the first exchange with a rounding error attached, and it would run, and it would have a shape, and the shape would be one member's.
- **The tone is on the box that holds the line and the figure, and nowhere else.** One fact in one channel: the group's name is not a signed quantity, and colouring it makes a falling basket read as an alert about the interface — the same mistake the genocide caption records, in miniature. It is also the channel that has to survive the fold, since the spine drops the percentage and the shape is all that is left. The direction tick is `display: none` in the rail for the same reason and one more: a glyph beside a tinted line is the same fact twice, and the exchange group has no tick to draw, so with the other six carrying one its label would have started 13px left of theirs.
- **The detail comes out of the rail's inner edge; the drawer could not come with it.** The drawer worked by sharing the top bar's two edges — which is what made it read as the bar opening rather than as something that happened near it — and it needed the canvas's whole width to hold `columns: 3`. A rail is 18–21rem and folds to 5.5. So the panel is a popover again, anchored to the **rail's** edge rather than to the pressed row's own left: at spine width the rows are 71px wide and four panels opened from four of them would each start three pixels apart. The row decides the top, the rail decides the side, and the top is clamped back inside the window because a group near the foot of a scrolled rail would otherwise open a panel whose bottom is off-screen. It is still the same non-modal `<dialog>` on `document.body`, which stops being a workaround for the scrubber's clipping and becomes the thing that lets it escape the rail's `overflow-y: auto`.
- **A folded instrument rail is not an empty one** (2026-08-02). It was: `--map-aside-w: 0px` and `.map-hud { display: none }`, so what a reader got back for folding was a 15×34 triangle stating nothing at all — not which layers were on, not what was shading the land, not what the world was doing — and the same press took away the only on-screen route to the time range, the layer toggles and the ground picker. That is a lot to spend on tidiness. It narrows to a **spine** now: `--map-spine-w`, 5.5rem, which is the widest group label (`currencies`, 11px at `--track-wide`) plus the reduced padding either side, measured rather than chosen. The controls go and the readings stay, because those are two different kinds of thing and only one of them was ever in the way. The label sits over its own line and the percentage drops — at 71px the shape and its tone are the whole readout. Three consequences worth stating: the two panes now fold *differently*, so `createPaneSeam` takes `verbs` and the right seam reads "Collapse"/"Expand" rather than "Hide"/"Show" — a button offering to show the instruments while four of them are on screen describes a layout that no longer exists; the seam **keeps straddling**, since it still has a pane on both sides of it, and leaving the aside in the "a folded seam sits wholly inside the window" rule would have pushed the one seam that is not at the screen's edge half a control away from the edge it marks; and the stored `aside.off` needs no migration, because it now restores something strictly more visible than what it recorded.
- **The `markets` chip stopped hiding the money block, and the old behaviour was right at the time.** `marketStrip.setVisible(layersOn.markets)` was in `applyLayerVisibility` on the reasoning that "the strip is the layer's readout, so it goes with the layer" — correct while it was chrome floating over the canvas, where a ranked list of exchanges above a map that draws none is a caption for a picture that is not there. In the rail it is not a caption. It is a block of readings, five sevenths of which — currencies, metals, crypto, and three world series — the `markets` layer has never drawn, and taking all of it away because the reader switched off thirty marks on the globe answers a question they did not ask. `setVisible` still exists and is still called from `loadLayers`; what it now reports is whether the **data** arrived, which is the one thing that should be able to empty this box.
- **The rails resize and fold, and folding is not "see more map".** On a height-bound globe reclaiming width reclaims no globe; what it reclaims is attention, and the code must not imply otherwise. What a drag actually adjusts is the split between two columns of *type* — the planet is a constant through it. Widths are written to `--map-rail-user` / `--map-aside-user`, which the stylesheet feeds into its own `clamp()`, never to the properties the layout reads: an inline override of those would replace the arithmetic outright and a width dragged on a 2560 monitor would arrive whole on a phone. Three things were each a bug first. The grip is **permanent**, not hover-revealed — a seam with no visible mark is a door with no handle, discoverable only by sweeping an edge nobody has reason to sweep. A folded pane's seam **stops straddling its edge**, or 7.5px of the only control that reopens it sits off-screen. And `preventDefault()` on `pointerdown` had to go: it suppresses the compatibility mouse events, so it silently killed the double-click that resets a pane to the layout's own width. Move and release listen on the *document* rather than relying on pointer capture, which is exactly the thing that fails at the edges.
- **The map opens on Makkah** (2026-08-01), not the point in the Libyan desert that framing had chosen. A globe has to be pointed somewhere and, unlike a flat map, cannot decline to choose — the half it faces is the half that exists. This site already keeps one frame (`MAKKAH_TZ` for the clock, the scrubber and the Hijri date; Umm al-Qura for the prayer lines), so the camera now agrees with the clock. It costs 27.8° of longitude: the hemisphere gains east and south Asia and loses the eastern Pacific.
- **The rail carried the day's five prayer times in Makkah, and they were removed.** The map draws `prayer-lines` and never says what they are, which is a real gap and this was the wrong answer to it: a column of five times has to be *for* somewhere, and a reader arriving at a news map has no reason to assume the rail means Makkah rather than where they are — so its first effect was to raise a question it answered nowhere on screen. The lines already carry the fact in the one form that cannot be misread, each drawn where it is true, with a hover readout that names and times it. This is a news map that keeps a Makkah clock, not a prayer-times app, and the difference is exactly a set of times for one place.
- **Genocide sits among the layers now, and the fence it needed on the strip is gone there.** On a row the only thing that could say "the controls end here" was a tick between chips; a column says it by construction — the mark is in the same 14px glyph column at the same rhythm, and it is a `<span>` with no hover and no pressed state, so it looks like a member of the group and behaves like a caption of it, which is the truth. Its hue reaches only the mark, as `--cat` does on a chip: on the whole item it was the one red *word* in a column of neutral ones, which made the gravest thing on the map read as an alert about the interface. The separator is targeted by a class (`is-genocide`), never by `:last-of-type` — the last `<span>` in `.map-filters` is the lock note, and a positional selector standing for an identity is how the next chip appended there silently moves a rule.
- **There is no 1:110m placeholder tier any more** (2026-07-25). It used to be the first-paint basemap with 1:50m swapped in past zoom 3.2 — but the map opens at world fit, around zoom 1.3, so the default view (the only one most readers ever see) was 110m and stayed there. 110m is not a rounded 50m: its generalisation deletes real geography — fjords, inlets, whole island groups — and the moment the border line became visible enough to read, coarseness was what it drew. Two fetches to reach the same place was the worse deal, so the placeholder is gone. The cost is one-off: `/basemap/*` is `max-age=86400, stale-while-revalidate=604800` and every URL carries the build's content hash, so it refetches only when the basemap changes. `.map-loading` ("Drawing the world…") covers the gap, because an empty dark canvas reads as breakage rather than as loading.
- **Country labels are gated on area, not just collision.** 50m carries 240 countries against 110m's 176, and the newcomers are mostly specks — unfiltered, the world view acquired PITCAIRN IS., NORFOLK ISLAND, NAURU and BERMUDA scattered across an empty Pacific, each as loud as BRAZIL, with nothing nearby for collision resolution to suppress them against. The `country-labels` filter steps the minimum `area` by zoom, starting at 0.00008 sr — where the old 176-country set ended — so the world view keeps its previous density and small states arrive as the camera earns them.
- **The loudest ink on this map was the names of the countries, and nothing else came close** (2026-08-02). Measured off the rendered canvas at the view the map opens on, bucketing every pixel by contrast against the ocean: of everything at **11:1 or better, 100% was neutral grey** — all of it this layer and `sea-labels`. Not one story beacon, conflict mark, famine glyph or genocide ring reached that band, and the top 1% of ink on the whole map came out 88% neutral. The subject of a news map was never the brightest thing on it. The load order says it out loud: `country-labels.geojson` is a few KB and `countries` is 1.6 MB, so for several seconds every reader gets **a globe made of nothing but country names** — no land, no coastlines, no borders, "Drawing the world…" still showing. It got there by stacking every emphasis channel typography has at once — `Noto Sans Bold`, uppercase, `text-letter-spacing: 0.14`, `labelDim` ink, and a `labelHalo` that is *darker than the ocean* and therefore a second contrast edge at **12.13:1** around every letterform — over a ground whose own encoding spans 1.36:1 end to end. Four of the five are spent down: Regular, 0.08, `text-halo-width` 1. **The ink is not**, and that is the constraint: `labelDim` measures 9.87:1 on the ramp's darkest stop and **4.90:1 on its brightest**, which is AA with 0.4 to spare, while `neutral` — the obvious step down — measures **2.76:1** there. Same wall the note on `MAP_COLOURS.label` hit from the other side. Measured after, at 1440×900: the ≥11:1 band fell from **5,656 px to 918 px and from 100% neutral to 79%**.
- **The density lever is `text-padding`, not the `area` gate.** Raising the gate from 0.00008 to 0.0003 drops 17 of 176 candidates and the seventeen are **Lebanon, Qatar, Cyprus, Kosovo, the Gambia, Trinidad, Jamaica and the Bahamas** — small states, which on a news map is not the same thing as unimportant ones, and a gate deletes a country at every zoom it covers. Padding thins by *collision* instead, so what it removes is crowding: `symbol-sort-key` already sorts on area, the large names survive a tight world view, and Lebanon arrives as the camera earns it. That is the arrangement the gate's own note describes and the gate cannot deliver. It is 12.
- **Beacons land on country names and always will, and that is the lesser evil** (2026-08-02). `story-points` is a `circle`, MapLibre's collision index holds **symbol layers only**, so a country label and a beacon cannot see each other. The fix exists and was built — a symbol layer drawing nothing, `icon-opacity: 0` (checked in `drawSymbols`, which skips the draw, while `Placement` never reads opacity), `icon-allow-overlap: true` so it always claims, `icon-ignore-placement: false` so the box lands in `this.grid` rather than `this.ignoredGrid`. It works, and it was reverted, because `map-island.test.js` already pins the opposite invariant on every mark layer: `icon-ignore-placement: true`, so that a mark "would push the basemap's own labels out of the way" is exactly what cannot happen. **MapLibre does not nudge a suppressed label, it deletes it** — country labels have a fixed point anchor and no `text-variable-anchor` — so the proxy trades a partly-covered name for no name. A covered label is still mostly readable. The answer to the complaint is the bullet above: a quiet name under a beacon stops reading as a collision.
- **Day/night is carried by the *lit* side over water** (2026-07-25). `night-shade` is black at 0.28, and the ocean is `#080a0d` — luminance 0.003 — so darkening it moves the colour by about two values in 255. The terminator was therefore invisible at sea: it read across the continents and stopped dead at the coastline, which made day and night look like a property of land rather than of the planet. There is no room to darken below near-black, so `dayPolygon` (`_map/solar.ts`, the same terminator ring closed over the opposite pole) feeds a `day-shade` fill inserted **before `land`** — the land layer paints over it, so it only ever reaches the water, and `--map-ground` and the CSS seam that depends on it are untouched. Which pole each hemisphere closes over is the whole correctness of it and fails silently if reversed, so a test pins it; note that at an equinox the terminator runs pole to pole and `terminatorLat` clamps to ±89.9, so the cap can only be identified by the closing vertex at *exactly* ±90.
- **Twilight, and the step function it replaced** (2026-08-02). `night-shade` was one fill at one opacity with a hard boundary, which made the map's entire lighting model a step: every point on the night side darkened by exactly the same amount, and day becoming night across zero degrees. A sphere lit by a step does not read as a lit sphere, it reads as a disc with a shape painted on it — most of why the globe felt flat while every individual layer measured correctly. `twilight` is a blurred band laid along the terminator, and it **lightens**, which is the same move `dayPolygon` already makes about water applied to the edge instead of the hemisphere: there is no room below near-black, so the ramp is cut upward out of the night. Deep night stays at 0.28 and every measurement taken against it still holds. **It is not the three real twilight caps, and that was checked rather than assumed**: civil, nautical and astronomical are spherical caps of radius `90° − |alt|` around the antisolar point, the longitude walk in `solar.ts` can only express a cap that spans every longitude, and that needs `|declination| >= |alt|` — true for 18° about two months a year and for 12° about five. A stack built that way would appear and disappear with the season, the night side visibly changing darkness for a reason no reader could recover. Drawing them properly means geodesic caps split at the antimeridian, which is the bar-across-the-map failure `prayer.ts` records.
- **`['zoom']` is legal only as the direct input of a top-level `step`/`interpolate`, and here it cost the whole map.** `line-offset` was written the obvious way — `['*', ['get', 'side'], ['/', width, 2]]` — which buries the interpolate two levels down. MapLibre rejects the **layer**, `addLayer` throws from inside `addDataLayers`, and every layer after it (the stories, the disasters, the conflict marks) was never added: a globe stuck on "Drawing the world…" with one line in the console. Same trap `story-place-count` records for its own `step`, one property along and far more expensive. The legal form puts the interpolate at the top and `['get', 'side']` in its *outputs*. **This is why `shoot.mjs` exists** — it captures `console` and `pageerror` per shot, and it is what found this; a hand-rolled static server did not, because the failure looks exactly like a slow fetch.
- **`scheduleMetric` waits on the source.** One `once('idle')` was enough against a 210 KB / 176-feature source; against 1.6 MB and 99k points parsed on a worker, a missed window leaves the world unshaded and *fully hatched*, which reads as "no data anywhere" rather than "still loading". It counted thirty idles for a while, which is the same race with a counter in front of it — see the 2026-07-26 note below.
- **MapLibre is not bundled into the island.** `scripts/build/islands.js` resolves the bare specifier to the copied vendor file, because the worker MapLibre spawns imports `maplibre-gl-shared.mjs` as a sibling regardless — inlining it shipped that chunk twice. The three `.mjs` files are copied verbatim, `modulepreload`ed from `templates/index.html`, and cached apart from the content cycle by the `/islands/*.mjs` rule in `_headers`.
- Stories are the only layer rebuilt as the scrubber moves: their decay alpha is per-feature and the cluster counts must reflect the filtered set. GDACS and conflict carry an event time and move by **`setFilter`**; layer toggles are **visibility**. Neither re-serialises GeoJSON per frame. Refreshes coalesce onto a rAF.
- Hover is **`promoteId` + `setFeatureState`**, read by a `['feature-state','hover']` paint expression — not a `setPaintProperty` rewrite per pointer move.
- **Nothing may write to a source on `idle` unless something changed** (2026-08-01). MapLibre draws a frame only when something has told it to, and `Map.setFeatureState` / `Map.removeFeatureState` both end in `_update()` whether or not they changed anything. `syncHoverState` and `syncPrayerHover` are on `idle` and each cleared its source unconditionally, so every idle tick dirtied a source, scheduled a render, and fired `idle` again. Measured headed on a real GPU that was **56.8 renders a second and ~57% of a core, indefinitely**, on a map showing no animation at all — a laptop fan spinning up on a static picture. Both now compare against what they last wrote (`hoverStateWritten` / `prayerStateWritten`) and return; the scrub path sets the story tracker back to null because it clears the state map itself, and `drawSolar` passes `rewrite` because the prayer line set changes shape across the year. Pinned by "an idle tick with nothing to change writes nothing to the map" in `map-island.test.js`, which asserts the *call* rather than the resulting state — a redundant clear leaves the state identical, which is exactly why nothing caught it for so long.
- **The map's idle cost cannot be measured headless, and "0 frames" is what a broken measurement also returns.** Two traps, both of which produced confident wrong answers before the real one: under SwiftShader a frame costs ~100× a GPU frame, so MapLibre's 300ms placement-recency window expires before the next frame lands and a repaint loop appears that does not exist on hardware; and an occluded or backgrounded window has `requestAnimationFrame` suspended entirely, so a page that is looping at 60fps reports zero. `scripts/perf/idle-renders.mjs` runs headed and opens every run with a control — drag the canvas, count frames — and reports failure rather than a number when the control does not clear 20fps. The companion instruments are `probe.mjs` (render errors, paint, weight, blocking, one page per template), `idle-profile.mjs` (CDP sampling profiler, self time by function) and `idle-trace.mjs` (which call site scheduled each frame or timer).
- **Boot is dominated by shader compilation, and that is upstream.** ~820ms of the map's ~1.9s of boot work is `getShaderParameter`: MapLibre calls `getShaderParameter(…, COMPILE_STATUS)` and `getProgramParameter(…, LINK_STATUS)` synchronously, which stalls the main thread until the driver finishes each program, and it does not use `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`. Nothing here can fix that without patching a vendored file, which would silently revert on the next version bump. **Re-checked against 6.1.0 (2026-08-01): unchanged — `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` still absent, and `Placement.setStale` still makes `hasTransitions()` true for a frame after every commit.** The number to compare on the next upgrade is the `getShaderParameter` self time in `idle-profile.mjs --settle 200`.
- **Nothing aggregates stories across places any more (2026-07-30).** The map clustered with supercluster (`clusterRadius: 30`, screen pixels) and drew each aggregate as a dark disc with a numeral in it. Measured at the world-fit zoom the map *opens* at: 61 groups, **47 discs and 14 individual beacons**, with **92% of the corpus (646 of 705) inside a cluster of ≥5**. The largest read `116` — 16.5% of everything — standing where no story stands, merging Washington, New York, Atlanta, Indianapolis and 23 more datelines; another merged London / Paris / Brussels / Geneva; another merged **Gaza, Cairo, Beirut and Damascus**. So the whole per-story alphabet this file argues for — coverage → radius, recency → alpha, the contested ring, the category hue — reached 2% of the marks on the default view, while `.map-key` decoded three channels that were not on screen. **A count is precise about a quantity and wrong about a place**: every aggregation across cities invents a coordinate, and this file spends paragraphs on printing Yafa and Al-Quds before dissolving Gaza into an anonymous disc with Damascus. And it could not be escaped — coordinates here are city-level, 445 of 705 stories share one exactly, so `expandCluster` offered a descent no amount of zooming could deliver. Clustering is off, `story-clusters` / `story-cluster-count` / `story-glow` are deleted along with `heatStops`, `clusterRadius`, `clusterStroke`, `countSize`, `clusterCategory`, `applyClusterScale` and `expandCluster` — about 320 lines — and `map-island.test.js` fails if a `cluster` key, a `point_count` filter or a layer named for one comes back.
- **Places are grouped by name *and* by distance, and positioned on a coordinate a story actually holds** (`public/islands/_map/places.ts`, pinned in `map-geo.test.js`). 705 points become **291 places**. Two relations, because neither works alone: the same dateline within `PLACE_SPLIT_KM` (120) absorbs Washington's 17 coordinates inside 2.2 km, and any dateline within `PLACE_SAME_KM` (5) merges the **eight coordinates that carry two spellings each** — `New Delhi`/`Delhi`, `Gaza`/`Gaza City`, `Sana'a`/`Sanaa`, `Odessa`/`Odesa`, all at byte-identical lat/lng, which keyed on the name alone would draw two numerals on one pixel: the exact overlap this change removes, reintroduced by the key. The tight radius has to stay tight — Ramallah is 15 km from Al-Quds and Bethlehem 9 km, different cities with different stories and, on this map, different peoples'. The name-first relation is what keeps **`La Paz`** two places 4,511 km apart. The position is the **modal** coordinate, ties to the newest story: a mean or a median of seventeen jittered Washingtons is exactly the invented coordinate the disc was wrong for, and a test asserts no place stands anywhere no story stands. `displayLocation` canonicalising those spellings upstream is the proper fix; the merge is here because here it is measured.
- **The glow is gone (2026-07-25), and the rule it left behind still holds.** Clusters used to stack four encodings of the same number on one coordinate — a cold→hot gold fill ramp, three blurred falloff rings, and a `heatmap` kernel-density layer beneath — on top of a numeral that already stated the count exactly. Over London, New York and Islamabad it read as a gold blob rather than a map. Removing `CLUSTER_HEAT`, `CLUSTER_RINGS`, `story-heat` and the light→dark label flip left colour meaning only category, which is the one thing the numeral cannot say. **Don't reintroduce a density field without a reason the count doesn't already cover.**
- **`story-density` is that reason, and it is one layer (2026-07-30).** Once a count can only honestly appear at a place that exists, **nothing on the map says how far the news reaches** — and extent is a shape, which no numeral states. It is also now the *only* density encoding: the discs, the gold ramp, the three rings and the label flip are all gone and stay gone, so this is a smaller map than the one that carried them. `conflict-marks` already proved the aesthetic works here — thousands of small squares over Ukraine, the Levant, the Sahel and Myanmar read as a heat pattern with no numerals at all. Two things earn it its place besides the argument. It is **raised from the places, not the stories**, so the wash and the numeral come from one aggregation and cannot disagree, and our own 1–2 km jitter cannot raise seventeen kernels where there is one city. And it is **inserted under `borders`** — a coastline draws straight through a patch, which makes the wash the only thing on this map with no edge, and an edge is how a reader separates it from a country shaded by the ground metric in the same neutral family. That is a difference of *kind*, the argument `nodataHatch` already makes; no choice of tone substitutes for it. Above `night-shade`, because night darkens the land and never the data. No `filter` and no toggle: this is the story layer's own alphabet, like a beacon's radius, and a chip for it would turn off part of a mark.
- **`GAUSS_COEF` is MapLibre's, and leaving it out costs a factor of 2.5.** The heatmap fragment shader is `weight * u_intensity * 0.3989 * exp(-4.5 * (d/r)^2)`, so the density accumulated at a kernel's centre is *not* `weight × intensity`. Calibrated against the product without it, every figure came out 60% low: the busiest place on earth sat barely past the ramp's first visible stop, which renders as a map with no field on it and nothing anywhere reporting a problem. It is written down in `places.ts` for that reason, and pinned. The same expression is why `heatmap-radius` reads as "where a place fades out" rather than "how big the patch looks" — at the full radius a place contributes 1.1% of its peak.
- **The wash is weighted `amax * sqrt(n)`, and the domain is fixed.** `sqrt` because counts per place run 1 to 62 and a linear weight either saturates Washington across half a continent — the gold blob in greyscale — or leaves a small place under the toe; it is the same reasoning `METRICS[key].scale` applies to the land. **Coverage is deliberately not in it**: `w` is absent on 62% of the corpus, and a radius can draw "unknown" as its own size while a continuous field cannot — a placeholder just makes a patch dimmer and reads as less news. Coverage is also a fact about media attention, and mixing it in would render a media fact as a geographic one. **Not rescaled to the visible set**, unlike the cluster domain it replaces: a disc radius only ranks marks within one view, but a field over geography is read as an absolute, so rescaling would draw a 5-story Washington at 24h identically to a 62-story one at 14d — the "same numeral, two different facts" objection that made `population` the default ground metric. A quiet day is allowed to look quiet.
- **The ramp's top is where the busiest *region* lands, not the busiest place.** `DENSITY_STOPS` tops out at 1.20, measured: Washington alone reaches 0.669 and sits mid-scale, which is right — one busy capital is not the loudest thing a fortnight of world news produces. The US northeast (Washington 62 + New York 22 + Atlanta 4, kernels overlapping at world zoom) reaches 1.238; London + Paris + Brussels reach 1.018. Anchoring on a single place would have clamped every one of those regions to the same tone. So a 10-story place lands at alpha 0.110, Washington at 0.244, London's neighbourhood at 0.307 and the US northeast at the 0.340 ceiling. Below 0.10 the ramp is fully transparent and `DENSITY_INTENSITY` is chosen so **one story accumulates exactly 0.085** — a lone story raises no field at all, being already completely expressed by its own beacon. **Stop 0 must be transparent**: MapLibre evaluates `heatmap-color` at every pixel of the layer's extent, so any alpha at density 0 paints the whole world, silently.
- **The kernel is wide on purpose, and that was a correction.** At 24px on a 512px world it was narrower than the gap between neighbouring capitals, so every busy place wore its own tidy disc — which reads as a *glow on the mark*, which is what was deleted in July. Washington and New York sit 11px apart at the opening zoom and London and Paris the same, so at 26–34px they pool into a region instead. The field only ever earns its place by describing one.
- **`MAP_COLOURS.density` is 216° at 5.4% saturation — less chromatic than a frontier.** The same hue as `border` and `prayer`, because blue-grey is already this map's furniture family, and a wash spanning continents is the last place to spend a hue. Composited over `LAND_RAMP[4]`, the brightest tone the ground can take, the faintest visible stop lifts it 1.26:1 and the peak 1.54:1, against the ramp's own largest internal step of 1.21:1 — so the wash's quietest visible tone is already outside the ramp's whole vocabulary. The first visible stop is deliberately *below* that bar: it is the onset of the scale, and there the structural guarantee carries it. **And there is a ceiling, which is the constraint nobody writes down until the map inverts**: the peak stays 1.24:1 *below* `labelDim`, the quietest ink drawn over it. Every label, beacon and glyph here is lighter than its ground — that is what the dark palette is for — so a wash bright enough to pass the ink would locally turn the map inside out. The corridor between `LAND_RAMP[4]` and `labelDim` is 1.90:1 and the field spends 1.54:1 of it; magnitude rides on how *wide* a patch is, not how bright.
- **The map opens on 3d** (2026-07-30). Not the full fortnight, for the reason it never did — the widest range is the one view where nothing stands out. And no longer 24h either: that measured **29 stories** against a real payload, and because the newest story is already hours old when a build runs, about *seven* twelve hours into a cycle. The rail read "29 STORIES" over a map that looked like nothing was happening, and seven points raise no field. 3d is 135. 24h is one press away.
- **There is one time range, at the head of the rail, and it governs everything below it** (2026-08-03). There were two, and only one of them had a control: the chips moved the beacons, while the money block's seven sparklines drew a hard-coded thirty observations that no gesture on this page could reach. A reader who set the map to 24h was still looking at six weeks of money. Two segmented controls four positions apart in one column, each governing half of what was under it, was the first fix and it lasted an afternoon — being told that the beacons heard you and the lines did not is an explanation of the implementation. So `RANGES` is **24h · 3d · 7d · 30d · 90d**, `.map-range-group` sits at `order: 1` under the clock and above the money, and `setRange` is the one place it is written: it syncs the chips, hands the strip `hours / 24`, fetches the archive when it needs to, resizes the rail and refreshes. Three call sites were writing those steps out separately, which is how the money block came to be missed by two of them.
- **The merge cost each side one thing and neither cost is silent.** The stories lost `14d`, which `30d` and `90d` contain; the money gained four steps and lost nothing, since 3d of daily closes is three points and draws. `null` went with `14d` — it meant "no lower bound" back when the corpus *was* the bound, and with real steps past the fortnight the bound has to be a number something can be fetched against. The opening step is the island's: `createMarketStrip` takes `rangeDays` rather than defaulting, because two defaults are a disagreement waiting for a first paint — the control reading `3d` over lines drawn across a month, reconciled only by a press.
- **A range past the fortnight needs stories the build does not ship, so there is an archive** (2026-08-03). `BUILD_WINDOW_DAYS` is 14 and `map.json` is that corpus; a control whose two widest positions drew the same beacons as its third would be lying about half of what it governs. `/api/map-archive.json` carries everything from the build window back to **90 days — 3,997 points, 251 KB gzipped** against `map.json`'s 726 and 47. **It is a separate endpoint precisely because of those numbers**: `map.json` is `<link rel=preload>`ed and is what the homepage blocks on, so folding ninety days in would put a quarter of a megabyte in front of first paint for stories most readers never ask for. Fetched on the first press of 30d or 90d and never before, which is the bargain `map-leads.json`, the conflict feed and the water layers already strike. Measured after: 40 stories at 24h, 159 at 3d, 380 at 7d, **1,586 at 30d and 4,687 at 90d**.
- **Two things about the archive are easy to get wrong and both were.** It is parsed **light** — `parseFrontmatter`, not `buildArticle`, which renders markdown and an isnad and a page, none of which reaches a beacon; 4,000 files measured at 411 ms against the seconds a full build would cost on each of five daily cycles. And its coverage is ranked through **the same `coverageRanks` percentile the fortnight is ranked by**, not its own: ranking the archive against its own distribution would make a radius mean one thing inside the fortnight and another outside it, on a map that draws both at once. The refresh path also had to stop dropping it — it was a bare `points = data.points`, correct while `map.json` was the whole corpus and a regression the moment anything older is merged in, because a reader on 90d who pressed refresh would watch three months of beacons collapse back to a fortnight.
- **The scrub rail widens for the two new steps and for nothing else.** It has spanned the whole fortnight at every range since it existed, and the shaded band is what says which slice of it is on the map — "the reader has no way to see that most of this histogram is not on the map" is the bug that band was added for. So `railStart()` is `max(rangeHours, BUILD_WINDOW_HOURS)`: the three ranges that fit inside the build window keep exactly the rail they have always had, and only 30d and 90d get a wider one. A rail that is always the range was the obvious alternative and fails on the geometry — at 24h against a 90-day axis the band is 12px of 1100, and shrinking the rail to the range instead deletes the band's whole subject.
- **The rail's day columns needed a stride the moment its span became an input.** It drew one hairline and one numeral per day unconditionally, which was correct by the span being a constant for as long as it was one. Ninety days across ~1100px is 12px a day and a 9px two-digit label is ~11px wide before its 3px offset, so every label overlapped its neighbour and ninety hairlines drew a picket fence where a calendar had been. 20px is the measured floor for a label plus offset and gap; the stride comes out 1 at a fortnight (unchanged), 2 at a month and 4 at a quarter. **The day under the scrub head is drawn whatever the stride** — it is the one column that answers "which day am I on", and losing it to an interval leaves the head the only thing on the axis moving with nothing to move against. The `aria-label` is derived from the span too; it said "the last 14 days" while the span was a constant, and a control that names a window it is not showing is worse to a screen reader than one that names none.
- **Getting back out**: a "whole world" control appears bottom-right once the view leaves home and hides when it returns; Escape resets the view when no card is open; the wordmark does the same. The URL never changes for any of it.
- **Refresh checks for new stories without discarding the view** (2026-07-26). At the head of the rail, beside the count. The map is a view the reader builds — a camera, a time slice, a set of categories, maybe an open card — and reloading to see whether anything broke throws all of it away. It refetches `/api/map.json` with **`cache: 'no-cache'`**, which is load-bearing: that endpoint is served `max-age=300`, so a plain fetch inside five minutes is answered from the browser's own cache and the button could never find anything. `no-cache` forces the conditional request and the endpoint does serve a **304 with an empty body**, so a check that finds nothing costs one round trip and no payload. Not `no-store`, which refetches every time. The initial load deliberately does *not* revalidate — that would defeat the `<link rel=preload>` and the stale-while-revalidate. `/api/map-leads.json` (85 KB) is refetched **only when something actually arrived**.
- **It never moves the reader.** At the live edge the scrub head follows the new end of the window; scrubbed back to Tuesday, they stay on Tuesday and the new stories are simply there when they come forward. When the window moves the whole scrubber is rebuilt (every tick, day label and histogram bucket derives from a fixed span) with the held position restored and clamped into the new rail. **`timeline.isLive()` is asked, never cached** — the island kept its own flag written from the scrubber's `onChange`, which fires on a gesture and nothing else, so before the reader had touched the scrubber the flag was an assumption; refresh read it as "scrubbed away", declined to follow, and left the new stories outside the visible slice while the button reported "+1 new". Found by driving the real map; pinned in `map-feed.test.js`.
- **The rail says which stories are on the map, and for a long time it said the wrong thing** (2026-07-30). Two encodings answer "which of these fourteen days am I looking at" and both were broken. The shaded band was `--text-dim` at `globalAlpha` 0.12 *under* the track's 0.6 wrapper — 0.072 of a grey over near-black, a rectangle that is not on screen — so the one mark connecting the range chips to the histogram could not be seen at all. And the bars split on `bucket <= headT`, which is *before or after the scrub head*, not *inside or outside the window*: at the live edge nothing is after the head, so on the default 3d view the rail drew eleven days of excluded stories at exactly the weight of the three included ones. The comment above it already stated the job correctly — "the reader has no way to see that most of this histogram is not on the map" — and the code did the other thing. Both now key on `windowStart … headT`, the band is 0.16 with a rule at its left edge (the right edge is the scrub head, so one rule closes the shape), and out-of-window bars step down to `--map-line` rather than fading, because those days are still the shape of the fortnight the window is a slice *of*.
- **The rail's canvas is painted in the map's palette, not the site's** (2026-07-30). `readPalette` reached for `--rule`, `--text-dim` and `--text` — the tokens for pages that follow the reader — on the one page that commits to dark whatever they prefer. `--rule` resolves to **`#181818`** here: 1.03:1 against the ocean and *darker than the land*, and it was painting every day column and every out-of-window bar. So the axis this component exists to provide ("a bare slider gives you no sense of *when*") was drawn in a colour that does not appear on screen. `--map-line` / `--map-ink-dim` / `--map-ink` now, and the day labels went from **1.51:1 to 4.69:1** measured off the rendered canvas.
- **The HUD and the scrubber are legible on the ground they actually sit on** (2026-07-30). The same audit gap the map's type had, one layer up: both scrims were tuned against the row nearest their own edge and abandoned every row past it. `.map-hud` ran 90% at the top to 55% at 60% of its height to transparent at the bottom — so on a three-row strip the layer chips had **DHUHR** printed through them and the ground legend carried **ASR** between "1.4B" and the key, and a HUD chip is 11px letterspaced caps exactly like a MapLibre place label. `.map-timeline` was transparent at the top of its own first line, which on the phone is the markets tally: **WEDDELL** through the exchange counts, **ANTARCTICA** through the day ticks. Both are pseudo-elements now, flat at 96% across the content and fading in an **overhang beyond the box** — which is the whole trick, because the heights are read (`--map-scrub-h` is the scrubber's `offsetHeight` and is what the peek card clears by; the legend used to hang at `top: 100%` off a measurement of the HUD, until it went in flow), so padding would have moved the panel and every peek card by the height of a gradient. 96 rather than 94: a bright label ghosts at 1.30:1 through 94 and 1.15:1 through 96. Not 100 — the fade is what says the map continues underneath, and a fully opaque strip is a toolbar.
- **The control says what it found, including nothing.** "nothing new" is the answer most presses deserve and the one a spinner alone never gives — without it a refresh that found nothing looks identical to one that failed. It is a glyph *and* a word: an unlabelled circular arrow reads as "reset" about as readily as "reload", and those would do opposite things to the view. The rail head is a `<div>` holding the disclosure and the refresh as siblings — a `<button>` inside a `<button>` is invalid and browsers drop the inner one, which would have left refresh unclickable on the phone layout, the one place the handle is a button at all.
- **Read stories grey out, and the record never leaves the device** (`_map/read-state.ts`). A story counts as read when its card opens — the reader committing to it, which is the only signal on this surface that means anything. It is one `localStorage` key holding slugs and nothing else: no timestamps, no counts, nothing that would make it a behavioural record rather than a list of what has been seen. The app-open beacon was removed in July for being a per-device record *on our server*; a read list is a far more revealing one, and the only basis on which this site can hold it is that we cannot see it. If a change ever makes it legible to the server, delete it instead. No cross-device sync, for the same reason. Capped at 600 slugs, oldest dropped — a slug aged out of the 14-day window can never be shown again.
- **Greyed in two channels, and said in words.** The ink steps down — an ink *step*, never `opacity`, which is the trick that put a filter chip at 2.33:1 and slipped past `colour-system.test.js` because opacity is not a colour literal — and the dot goes from a disc to a ring, so the state survives a reader who cannot separate two greys, in the vocabulary the map already uses (filled means live, hollow means not). The category reaches the dot as an inline `--cat`, the same way HUD chips take their layer's colour, so the stylesheet can hollow it without naming a hue. A `.sr-only` "(read)" carries it to anyone listening. **Greyed, never hidden or reordered**: the rail captions the map, and dropping a story the reader has seen would leave a beacon with no row to match it.
- Interaction: **hover never moves the camera.** Hovering a story previews it; committing is a click, from the beacon or the rail. The old 320ms dwell-to-fly dragged readers somewhere they hadn't asked to go whenever the pointer crossed a dense area, and clusters lost their hover-expansion for the same reason before losing the cluster too. Hovering a disaster/strait/conflict mark opens the sheet in its non-modal **peek** mode and a click pins it. The wordmark resets the view instead of reloading the homepage (the `href` stays, so modified clicks and a JS-less browser still navigate).
- **Peek and pinned are two densities, not one card shown twice.** Peek answers the single question a resting pointer is asking — how bad, how far off normal, how many dead — and stops; pinned carries prose, provenance, the series behind the number and related coverage. Peek must stay short: `.map-sheet.is-peek` caps at 55vh and clips rather than scrolls.
- **The peek card clears the scrubber by measurement** (2026-07-30). It had `bottom: 5.5rem` — the same hardcoded 88px `.map-reset` was fixed away from, wrong for the same reason and by more, because the card's bottom edge is pinned at that offset regardless of its own height. The scrubber is 97px at 1920 and was 203px at 920, so *every* peek card landed inside it at *every* desktop width: 9px of overlap at 1920, 29 at 1400, 51 at 1100, 93 at 1000, **115 at 920** — a card lying over the money ribbon and the clock. `left` and `width` derive from `--map-rail-w` too; the second hardcoded copy of the rail's width had already drifted a rem from the first. **`--map-scrub-h` is published on `body`, not on `.map-root`** — `createSheet` appends the dialog to `document.body`, so a property on the container never reached it, and pointing this rule at `--map-scrub-h` while it lived there would have silently resolved to the fallback and changed nothing. `body.map-page` is where the phone layout's three coupled heights already live; teardown has to remove it explicitly now, since clearing the container no longer does it. (`--map-status-w` was the second of these and is gone with the top strip.)
- **One Earth.** `renderWorldCopies` is off and the opening zoom is derived (`worldFitZoom` → `log2(width / 512)`), so a single world fills the canvas instead of repeating. `worldFitZoom` is also the map's `minZoom`, re-applied on resize — a world narrower than its canvas is not letterboxed by MapLibre, it is *duplicated*, and the old 2.4 ceiling put the world at 2702px, so any wider viewport got a second Australia at the right-hand edge. Coverage wins over framing. `onResize` must call `map.resize()` too: `applyPadding` and `worldFitZoom` only *read* dimensions, so without it the canvas kept its build-time size and the map drew into a corner of its own frame. `applyPadding` measures the rail's *actual* intersection with the canvas — they're separate grid columns and haven't overlapped since the layout became a grid, so the old unconditional rail-width inset pushed the map sideways and clipped a continent.
- Beacon size is a **percentile rank over the window's coverage figures, computed in `build.js`** — raw `eventCoverage` is absent on ~65% of articles and holds occasional nonsense (values in the tens of thousands), so a log curve left most of the corpus at the minimum radius and a handful of bad rows saturated. Stories with no figure get a fixed neutral size, which says "unknown" rather than "smallest".
- **The beacons grew when they became the only story mark** (2026-07-30). They were 2.6–6px on an alpha floor of 0.35, sized for a map where a beacon was the exception — 14 of 705 at the opening view. Now **3.4–7.5px on a floor of 0.45**: `glyphs.ts` had already established that 3.2px is below the floor at which a mark survives, so the subject of this map was drawn under it, and 7.5px keeps a beacon just inside the 16px `GLYPH_BOX` the overlays share so a well-covered story never out-sizes a Red GDACS alert. Unknown coverage lands at 4.55px, still below the known median of 5.41px, so the "unknown ≠ smallest" rule holds with more room. A week-old beacon now measures 1.89:1 against the ocean where it measured 1.57:1.
- **The rim is a second channel, not a detail, and the wash is why.** A `politics` fill measures **1.39:1** against the density peak over bright land — nearly the same luminance — while its ocean-coloured rim measures **3.73:1**. Over water it inverts: the rim collapses to 1.84:1 and the fills run 2.83–4.60:1. So fill carries the mark on dark ground and rim carries it on bright, the rim went from 0.6px/0.6α to **1px/0.85α**, and the pinned invariant is the *better* of the two on every ground the wash can lie on — the same reasoning this file already applies to the overlay glyphs' halo. The worst case is `politics` on the darkest ramp stop at 2.29:1, where what still makes the mark a mark is the edge *between* rim and fill, which no ground can affect.
- **A stack of beacons is sorted, and says so.** 445 of 705 stories share a coordinate, so 2 to 62 beacons composite at those places. `circle-sort-key: ['get', 't']` makes the top of a pile the latest story there rather than whichever parsed last — which is also what makes hovering a pile preview the story its numeral stands for. The stack going opaque is honest; the exact fact it cannot state is the numeral's job and the place card's.
- Decay half-life is **72h** (`_map/types.ts`), sized to this map's 14-day window. The 18h curve borrowed from mobile's 72-hour globe put 85% of the corpus at the alpha floor and collapsed recency to "today or not".
- **Water is on the map** (2026-07-28): lakes, rivers and marine labels, from three files that had sat unused in `shared/data/` since the start while mobile read all three. Sizes decide the treatment. **Seas are 54 label points, 1 KB gzipped** — the cheapest thing in that directory by two orders of magnitude — and they load with the style, because the eleven chokepoints (Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, Suez, Malacca, Bosporus, Gibraltar, Kerch) all sat in water the basemap left as anonymous black. Being labels they add no line family to a map that has already had to ration lines.
- **Lakes and rivers are idle-deferred, because GeoJSON is not TopoJSON.** 52 KB and 54 KB of source become **104 KB and 101 KB on the wire** — shared arcs get expanded — so together they are 205 KB against the coastline's 546 KB, a **37% heavier first paint** for detail nobody opens this map to see. Simplification does not recover it: lakes start to show it before the saving is material and rivers barely respond at all, their vertices already being sparse. So the fix is *when*, not how much — `loadWater` in `situation-map.ts`, one request for both, the same treatment the conflict feed and the lead sentences get. The sources are declared empty in the style and filled by `setData` with a **parsed object**, since MapLibre 6 dropped the URL form and a string makes no request and throws nothing.
- **There is no colour that works flat for a river, and that is why `water` is its own token.** `ocean` is the obvious choice — a river is the same substance as the sea — and it measures **1.04:1 against `LAND_NO_DATA`** and 1.21:1 on the ramp's darkest stop, so it would be absent across the thirty hatched countries and most of the dark half of every metric. Any mid-tone that survives that collides with one of the ramp's own five stops; the best flat candidate a search could find still measured **1.10:1 against `border`**, which is a river indistinguishable from a frontier. Hue cannot break the tie either — `border` and `prayer` are **both at 216°**, because blue-grey is already this map's furniture family. So the separation is saturation: `#4a7fae` at 40% against `border`'s 10%, the same argument and the same 20-point floor the genocide tone uses against conflict, holding a 1.92:1 minimum across all six tones the land can be painted. Rivers, lake rims and the marine labels all take it, so everything about water is one family.
- **Lakes fill in `ocean` and sit above the land, under the borders.** Above the land because a lake is water carved out of it, and the map's strongest ground rule is that the darkest thing is water; under the borders because a frontier down the middle of the Great Lakes, Victoria, Chad or the Caspian is a fact about the lake and has to stay drawn. The fill alone is not enough at 1.04:1 on a hatched country, so the rim in `water` is what guarantees it reads — and it is the same thread the rivers are drawn in, so a river running into a lake continues as its edge. Lakes take the night wash but not the day lift, since `day-shade` is inserted before `land` to keep it off the ground.
- **Both layers are gated on significance, and the first guesses were wrong in both directions.** Lakes step on `area` like `country-labels`: the world gate is **0.0002 sr, which is 21 lakes** — Superior down to Turkana, the set a reader would name. It was 0.00004 first, which admitted **110** and speckled Fennoscandia and the Canadian Shield with reservoirs and IJsselmeer at sizes well under a pixel. Rivers step on Natural Earth's `scalerank`: 27 at world, all 255 by z6. Ungated the world view is a net of hairlines over every continent — the cluster-glow mistake in another medium. Width is constant per zoom rather than per rank, because a river is not a quantity.
- **`sea-labels` is the last layer in the base style, and that is load-bearing.** MapLibre walks symbol layers top-down and the *later* layer claims its collision boxes first, so under `country-labels` this was the lowest-priority symbol layer on the map: it drew only where nothing else wanted the space — Labrador Sea, Sea of Okhotsk, Philippine Sea, all empty ocean — while the Mediterranean, Red Sea, Arabian Sea and Caribbean went unnamed. Those are exactly the waters the layer was added for, crowded precisely because that is where the news is. Last, it claims before the country and city names, which is the right way round rather than merely the effective one: a sea label is placed at the centroid of open water, so where it competes with a land label at all, it is the land label that has drifted out to sea. It still loses to everything `addDataLayers` adds — the stories are the subject, the basemap is the ground.
- **It has no `minzoom`, and that is the point.** `worldFitZoom` is `log2(width / 512)` and is also the map's floor, so the default view sits at **1.11 on a 1104px canvas and −0.39 on a phone**. A `minzoom` of 1.4 — which this had — hid the marine labels at the one view every reader starts from. Density is the rank filter's job, never a zoom floor's.
- **Persian Gulf and Sea of Japan keep Natural Earth's names, and that is a decision.** Both are live disputes (against Arabian Gulf and East Sea) and both are the UN and IHO terms, where each alternative is one party's claim — the same reasoning that keeps Western Sahara as it is, and a different case from Palestine, where the site follows a UN determination rather than departing from one. `map-geo.test.js` pins both in **both directions**, so neither the name nor a well-meaning rename can land silently. `Bahía de Campeche` is likewise left in Spanish.
- **Every file the basemap is built from belongs in `BASEMAP_V`.** The three new sources were added to that hash the same day they were added to the build: `/basemap/*` is `max-age=86400`, so a layer left out of the hash is a layer that goes stale for a day with no way for a reader to force it — which is exactly how the map went on printing "Tel Aviv" after the build had started emitting "Yafa".
- **Cartography of historic Palestine**: `scripts/build/basemap.js` merges the Natural Earth "Israel" and "Palestine" geometries with topojson `merge()` — a topological union, so the shared arc is dissolved rather than stroked twice — and labels the result Palestine (ISO2 `PS`, so a click opens that profile). Place labels run through the same `displayLocation` table the articles use, so the basemap prints Yafa and Al-Quds rather than contradicting the story drawn on top of it.
- **Territories carry the name of the people whose land they are** (`shared/place-names.ts`, so the map, the country pages and the app can't disagree): Malvinas, Kanaky and Kalaallit Nunaat, stated outright rather than parenthetically — the same house style as Palestine and Yafa. Western Sahara stays as it is: that *is* the UN's term for a Non-Self-Governing Territory, and the alternative is Morocco's "Southern Provinces". Puerto Rico stays too — the test is what the people concerned call it, and renaming it to Borikén on their behalf would fail that test. `scripts/lib/map-geo.test.js` pins all of this against the built labels, because `displayCountryName` is easy to bypass by reading `properties.name` straight off Natural Earth.
- Sources that disagree sharply about a story (`sentimentDivergence` ≥ 0.35) get a contested ring; chokepoints size on the signed magnitude of `delta7vs90`, not a binary threshold.
- Conflict recency anchors on the **dataset's newest event**, not `Date.now()` — UCDP publishes months in arrears, and decaying against wall-clock renders the whole layer at the opacity floor. Its 260 KB payload and the lead sentences are both `requestIdleCallback`-deferred.
- **Genocide is its own layer, above every other** (2026-07-25). Situations a named UN body has *determined* to be genocide, drawn as a heavy bone-white ring with a solid core and the place name always set beside it — added last so nothing can cover it, and `-allow-overlap` throughout because a mark suppressed for collision reads as an absence. It carries **conflict's hue at the saturation conflict deliberately lacks** (`#f5372b` against `#c05252`) — same subject, far end of it, so a reader who has learned that red means people being killed does not need a second vocabulary. A test asserts the tone is unique, red, and at least 20 saturation points clear of every other overlay. It has **no time filter** — a determination is a condition, not an event, so the scrubber must not hide it — and **no toggle**, unlike the three feed layers. It is **named at the end of the filter row, past its own separator, not in `.map-key`** (2026-07-26): that group's `aria-label` was "What the beacons mean" and its other three items decode a channel a story beacon spends ink on (radius, alpha, the contested ring), which genocide is not — at the head of it the gravest mark on the map read as a fifth way of encoding a beacon. The move also puts it beside `conflict`, which is the whole point of its colour: same hue, far end of the saturation, a relationship only legible with the two chips adjacent. It stays a `<span>` among four `<button>`s, and the separator is what says the controls end there. **This is why it stayed on the strip when the rest of the legend folded** (2026-07-30, see "The strip carries controls"): the beacon key and the ground note went into the panel, and moving genocide with them would have separated it from `conflict` again and thrown away the argument its colour is making. Adjacency outranks the 77px. The record is `shared/genocide.ts`; the card leads with the body that made the finding rather than a casualty figure, because the first question about such a mark is who is saying it.
- **Famine is the map's second determination layer** (2026-07-30). The IPC — Integrated Food Security Phase Classification — is a process in which a national Technical Working Group classifies a subnational area into one of five phases, on a date, from evidence it publishes. That is the same kind of object as `shared/genocide.ts` and it is drawn on the same terms: a condition rather than an event, so **no time filter** — `map-island.test.js` fails if one appears — and a card that leads with who made the finding and when. It came out of the military-tracking search above, and it is the better addition on this map's own terms: **CC0, keyless**, from OCHA's Humanitarian Data Exchange, covering exactly where this site's coverage centres. `scripts/fetch-ipc.js` → `content/.ipc.json` → `/api/ipc.json`, arithmetic in `scripts/lib/ipc.js`, pinned in `ipc.test.js`. 105 areas across 7 countries, 34 KB.
- **The phase is read, never derived, and that is the whole layer.** IPC classifies an area on population *thresholds* — roughly 20% in Phase 3+ puts the area in Phase 3 — not on which phase holds the most people. Deriving it from the population columns by largest share puts **2,122 of 2,804 areas in Phase 1**, measured: a famine layer drawing the world as mostly fine, with nothing failing and nothing to see. `overall_phase` exists and is the only phase the module accepts. **Which is why two files are required**: the published CSV carries the analysis date, the window dates and the populations and *no phase and no geometry*; the per-country GeoJSON carries `overall_phase`, `confidence_level` and geometry and **no dates at all**. Neither makes a mark alone. They join on the normalised area name, which was the risk in the design and turned out to be exact — **100% across all 25 countries fetched**, 0 unjoined. Watch `overall_phase: 0`: it is IPC's "not analysed", not a low phase, and Nigeria publishes **220 of its 795 areas** at it.
- **The bar is compound, and Gaza is why.** `overall_phase >= 4` — Emergency and Catastrophe — is the intended bar and is 101 areas across six countries. It also **excludes Gaza**, whose four areas the November 2025 analysis classifies at **Phase 3** while counting 39,885, 37,950, 24,080 and 1,885 people in **Catastrophe** in them: an area phase is a threshold on the whole assessed population, so a district can hold a Catastrophe caseload and classify below it. Correct use of the scale, useless bar for a news map. So an area is drawn at Phase 4+ **or** where the IPC counts anyone in Phase 5 — which is not the derived phase the first rule forbids, since nothing is re-classified and the mark still carries `overall_phase`. It adds exactly Palestine; Sudan's fifteen Catastrophe areas are already Phase 4. And because the mark would otherwise read as the *mildest* on the layer, the card says so in words wherever the phase and the caseload disagree.
- **The vintage is the quantity, not a lapse flag.** IPC current windows run two to three months and analyses re-publish every few months, so at any instant **49 of 3,096** areas have a window bracketing today — a "lapsed" flag would be true of 98% of the layer, which is a constant, not a flag. What varies is the **age of the analysis**, 0 to 12 months across the gated set (Somalia 3.9, Sudan 6.9, Gaza 8.9, Afghanistan 9.9), and that rides on `icon-opacity` with a high floor, the way the conflict layer decays on its own dataset's recency rather than on wall-clock. The twelve-month gate is load-bearing: the file still carries **Ethiopia's May 2021 analysis** in its *current* columns, and those areas hold the three largest Phase 5 populations in the dataset, so ungated any "worst areas" read puts 2021 Ethiopia above 2026 Gaza. The projections are recorded and deliberately **not drawn** — a projection has no published `overall_phase`, so drawing one means deriving a classification or pairing this period's phase with a later period's numbers, which is worse because it looks right. Whether one covers today is one line on the card, which is the difference between a classification that was extended and one that ran out.
- **It is a mark layer, not a land tint, and the payload is why it could be either.** Three things already shade the land — `LAND_RAMP`, the density wash and `nodataHatch` — and the wash earns its place by being inserted under `borders` so it is the only thing on the map with no edge. A phase-classified polygon has an edge and would read as a country shaded by the ground metric. The cost of settling that argument was already paid by the payload: the 52 country GeoJSONs are **63.1 MB**, against a 1.6 MB basemap and a lakes-plus-rivers pair judged a 37% first-paint penalty at 205 KB. So geometry is reduced at build time to one representative point per area, through `representativePoint` in `scripts/lib/geo-point.js` — which is `largestPolygonCentroid` lifted out of `country-pages.js`, plus a `Point` passthrough, because the IPC's collections **mix geometry types**: Somalia is 63 Points beside 44 polygons and the points are the urban and IDP caseloads, which is most of its Phase 4 set. The trade is stated: the place is named, its extent is not drawn.
- **Violet, at 269°, and the hue is the argument.** Every other overlay is warm — gdacs and thermal 27°, straits 44°, conflict and genocide 0–4° — with one teal and one sage, so a warm famine mark lands inside the family that already means violence or hazard, and a reader who has learned red means people being killed must not unlearn it for a classification about food. 269° is **87° from its nearest neighbour**, the largest separation the wheel still had, and clear of the 216° blue-grey the furniture occupies; a test pins both distances. It is deliberately **not the IPC's own five-phase palette**: recognisable, and its top two stops are the red this map has spent on conflict and genocide, with Phase 5's dark red landing inside the 20-saturation-point corridor `map-geo.test.js` reserves for the genocide mark. `glyphs.ts` already says how to avoid paying that — **once the silhouette identifies the layer, the phase can ride on shape** — so the glyph is a three-block column filled from the bottom, outlined so it reads as one-of-three rather than as three marks of different sizes, and drawn at 10–14px rather than 7 because each block has to survive on its own and 105 marks can afford the room.
- **Thermal anomalies are corroboration, not a fire map** (2026-07-29). NASA FIRMS publishes active-fire detections — one row per satellite pixel where a VIIRS pass measured infrared radiance above the background — and the honest reading of one is narrow: *an instrument recorded heat here, at this power, at this minute.* It cannot say what was burning. So the layer draws a detection only where a story we published stands beside it, which makes each mark a second transmitter for an event that already has a chain of sources. `scripts/fetch-firms.js` → `content/.firms.json` → `/api/firms.json`, with the arithmetic in `scripts/lib/firms.js` and pinned in `firms.test.js`. Needs a free `FIRMS_MAP_KEY`; without it the stage logs a skip and the layer is absent.
- **Three gates, and the third one *is* the layer.** Measured against a real snapshot: the fetcher's AOI grid yields 1,391 clustered anomalies within 75 km of a story location, the time window takes that to 58, and the subject gate takes it to **11**. Without that third gate the map published a veld fire 36 km outside Johannesburg cited against *"Joburg Bills Wrong Owners"* — nine of them — plus fires hung off "Campaign Targets Cookie Banners", "Fossils Show Inbred Cats" and "Korean Traders Drive Shiba Surge". A story's `location` is a city, so a 75 km radius catches every fire in a metropolitan region and attaches it to whatever we happened to write about that city: the `fetch-ioda.js` failure in a new costume, a join that could not separate "our story is about this fire" from "our story is about this city". `THERMAL_VOCABULARY` is the fix and is editorial, like `STATE_OUTLETS` — **things that burn, and violence that sets things alight.** Matched against title and concepts, *not* the body, which admits a passing mention and takes 11 to 19. `strike` is never matched bare: it is the most valuable word in the list and the most dangerous, since "Strikes Kill Iraqi Paramilitaries" is exactly the story a thermal mark corroborates and "Rail Workers Strike Over Pay" is exactly the story it must not, so it is only ever matched in phrases a labour dispute cannot produce. `plant` was tried and removed for reading botanical as often as industrial.
- **Persistence is the only flare filter there is.** The NRT products **drop the `type` column** that classifies a static land source, so nothing upstream identifies a gas flare, a smelter or a volcano — and those burn every night, in exactly the places the news is. The filter is therefore a 5-day baseline in one request (`dayRange` is capped at 5, which is the whole reason to ask for more than one day): a ~1.1 km bin alight on four of five days is an installation. Bins, not pixels, because the same flare does not land on the same 375 m pixel on consecutive passes and a finer grid would read one steady source as five one-day ones. The naive version of this rule deletes every large wildfire on its fourth day, which is what `escalating` exists for — a persistent bin is kept when this pass burns more than twice its own per-day baseline. Distinct *dates*, not detections, or a busy overpass schedule makes everything look permanent.
- **The mark shares the disaster hue on purpose.** `thermal` is `gdacs`'s 27° one lightness step up (.57 against .48), because it is the same subject seen by a different kind of witness — the way `genocide` is the far end of `conflict` — and `glyphs.ts` earns the reuse by putting identity in the silhouette. The step is not decoration: a GDACS wildfire alert and its own thermal footprint are *the same coordinate*, and in one tone that pair reads as a single object with a strange outline. Saturation is .65 against a hard ceiling of ~.71, because the genocide test requires every other overlay to sit 20 points below it — the hotter, more obvious oranges (`#e8632f` at .80, `#e0713a` at .73) both fail there, which is that test keeping the one unmuted tone unspent. The glyph is a core with four **diagonal** rays: on the axes it reads as a plus, which is a medical mark in every vocabulary a reader brings, and the gap between core and rays is what stops it being a blob at the 7px it shares with a story beacon.
- **Its legibility is measured against the ocean and its own halo, never the land ramp.** A 3:1-on-any-ground bar was written first and failed at 2.98:1 on the ramp's lightest stop — and then measurement showed the *whole* overlay set comes in under 3:1 there: gdacs 2.21, conflict 1.77, genocide 2.12, straitsSurge 2.67, with thermal's 2.98 the third best figure on the map. What makes these marks read on a bright country is `icon-halo-color: labelHalo`, which is what `MAP_COLOURS.neutral` means by the halo making "the ground stops being a variable at all". So contrast against the halo is the real invariant, and a ramp-stop bar is a standard this design holds nothing to.
- **Size is log FRP, opacity is the instrument's own confidence, and the scrubber moves it.** Radiative power runs from 5 MW to 17,000 across a real snapshot, so a linear radius draws one Siberian fire the size of a continent and everything else at the floor. Confidence gets the opacity channel because VIIRS marks a detection low where sun glint or a weak (<15 K) anomaly could account for it, and a reading the satellite is unsure of must not arrive at the same weight as a saturated night pass. A pass happened at a moment, so `thermal-marks` joins `applyTimeFilters` — unlike the straits and the exchanges, which are statements about now, and unlike genocide, which is a condition. Drawn **above the stories**, for the market layer's reason: these sit within 75 km of a beacon by construction, and a cluster survives a burst crossing it where a single anomaly, covered, is simply absent.
- **The card states what the instrument saw and stops.** Peek answers the resting pointer — power, pixel count, distance, how long the place has been alight. Pinned adds the caveat in words (a strike, a wildfire, burning cropland and a flare are the same reading), the provenance line, and a deep link to the pass on NASA's own map so the reader can check us rather than take it from us. The hero prints **"24.9 km from the story"** because the join is 75 km wide and the card has to admit it — the mark is never claimed to be *at* the story. The chip's `title` carries the short form of the same caveat, the way `PRAYER_NOTE` and `HIJRI_NOTE` do.
- **`content/.firms.json` is wider than what is published**, holding all 1,391 distance-bounded anomalies while `/api/firms.json` carries the 11 that can be explained. Same treatment `fetch-ioda.js` gives its series: the evidence stays inspectable, and only what can be accounted for is drawn. `skipped` counts what went and why — persistent bins, sub-floor clusters, anomalies beyond the radius, anomalies with no relevant coverage — because a bounded layer that does not say what it left out reads as complete coverage.
- **What was refused, and why** (from the same 2026-07 review of open-source military-tracking sources). **ADS-B aircraft**: a 1 Hz cooperative feed has no snapshot form on a 5×-daily static build, the honest limit is that you see the logistics tail rather than combat types, and several ADS-B communities self-impose delays on publishing force movements during active conflict — this site covers Gaza and Yemen, and near-real-time military logistics is a materially different editorial act from reporting on it. **GNSS interference (GPSJAM)**: conceptually the best fit, being an area condition over the chokepoints already drawn, but its upstream is adsbexchange.com, which restricts republishing, and it has no documented API. **Satellite imagery change detection**: that is imagery intelligence, a different publication with no chain of transmission — we would be the primary source, unreviewed, drawn at the confidence of a UN determination. **AIS**: already held in its better form, since the chokepoint series is an AIS-derived aggregate with a named institution behind it. One fact worth keeping if orbits ever come up: the catalogue exhausted 5-digit NORAD numbers on 2026-07-11, so ingest OMM/JSON and never write a TLE parser. **Re-asked and re-refused on 2026-07-30**, this time with figures rather than reasoning, because "is there an API for carriers and PLA/Russian movement" is the question a news map invites. The free military ADS-B feed (`opendata.adsb.fi/api/v2/mil`) returned **286 aircraft, 200 positioned: 42% continental US, 49% western Europe, 3 in the whole East Asia box and 2 in the Middle East**, composed mostly of T-6 trainers, EC145 medevac helicopters and King Airs — a map of American and European *training* flights, under a personal-non-commercial licence. PLA and Russian aircraft do not transmit ADS-B and there are no volunteer receivers there in any case. **AIS gained a second and stronger objection**: it is unauthenticated, and naval positions have been actively *fabricated* — HMS Queen Elizabeth's strike group, ~100 naval vessels including 11 Royal Navy, NATO ships off Sevastopol — so on a site whose architecture is a chain of transmission it is not a weak source, it is an adversarial input. Paid feeds (MarineTraffic, Datalastic) forbid republishing outright. **Carriers specifically have no API at any price**; the public record is USNI News's weekly Fleet and Marine Tracker, a copyrighted article with a chart image, which is a thing to cite in a story and not a layer. And **the NGA's maritime broadcast warnings were checked and rejected on measurement, not principle** — the endpoint is real, public and keyless (`msi.nga.mil/api/publications/broadcast-warn?status=active&output=json`, 386 active records) and only **9 are military**, several of them "until further notice" from 2023; of the 18 within 300 km of a chokepoint we already draw, almost all are a destroyed buoy, a dangerous wreck, a RACON or a gas leak, and the two genuinely military ones are Black Sea mining notices from 2022 with no expiry, which would have rendered as current. **ACLED is separately and permanently closed to this site**: its EULA is non-commercial and states it is "not sufficient for Licensed Content to simply be supplemented, appended, excerpted, reorganized, or made available through Licensee's own dashboard", so the obvious fix for UCDP publishing months in arrears cannot be used. What came out of that search instead was the famine layer below.
- **Markets are 30 exchange marks, and the set is the point** (2026-07-25). One mark per stock exchange at its city: the sign of the day's move picks the colour, the size of the move drives radius and stroke weight, and a fill appears only while the exchange is actually trading — so an open market is a disc and a closed one a ring, the vocabulary the map already uses. Under 0.15% the mark stays neutral rather than claiming a direction it hasn't got. The colours are **`--map-pos`/`--map-neg`, not new ones** — the site already had a pair meaning "a signed change", and a seam test pins them to `OVERLAY_COLOUR.marketUp`/`.marketDown` because the marker is painted by MapLibre and its sparkline by CSS. `_map/chart.ts` grew a `palette: 'signed'` switch for exactly that: a falling index drawn in chokepoint gold would borrow a meaning it does not have.
- **Shape says what, colour says which way** (2026-07-26). Every mark on this map was a circle, and `market-marks` and `chokepoint-marks` had drifted into *byte-identical* paint expressions — same radius domain, same stroke domain, same neutral. Hue could not rescue it either: `economy` `#d0a24a` and `straits` `#c9a84c` are three points apart and `politics`/`conflict`/`gdacs` are one red family. So colour was spending itself on identity and failing, with nothing left over for value. Now the silhouette carries the layer — hazard triangle, strait channel, conflict square, market tick — and colour is free to mean direction again. The alphabet is `_map/glyphs.ts`: shapes authored as vertex tables in a 16-unit box, rasterised to **real signed-distance fields** and handed to `map.addImage(..., {sdf: true})`. It has to be a true distance field, not an alpha mask: MapLibre's shader cuts at `alpha = 0.75` and reads halos outward from it, so a mask renders convincingly and then aliases under scale and silently ignores `icon-halo-width`. Font glyphs were the obvious alternative and are not available — Geometric Shapes is range `9472-9727` and `public/basemap/fonts/` ships four ranges, none of them that one, with no generator in the repo.
- **Only one layer actually adopted it until 2026-07-29.** The paragraph above described the intended state, not the shipped one. `glyphs.ts` authored the shapes, `addImage` registered every one of them, and the HUD chips rendered them as SVG — while `gdacs-marks`, `chokepoint-marks` and `conflict-marks` stayed `circle` layers that referenced no image. `market-marks` was the only `icon-image` on the map. So four chips promised a silhouette their own layer did not draw, which is the legend-drifting-from-the-mark failure the module was written to end, running backwards, and nothing failed: the images were valid, the chips were right, and nothing asked whether anything used them. All three are symbol layers now, and `map-island.test.js` walks what the island hands the engine and fails if a registered glyph is drawn by no layer. **Both collision flags are mandatory on every one of them.** `icon-allow-overlap` because `queryRenderedFeatures` returns only *placed* symbols, so a collided mark is not merely invisible, it is unhoverable and its card can never open; `icon-ignore-placement` because a circle layer was never in the collision index at all, and without it several thousand conflict marks start deleting the country and place names beneath them. Radius domains translate as `wantedCssPx / GLYPH_BOX` — a circle of radius r is a mark 2r wide — except the conflict square, which takes `glyphs.ts`'s own stated 5–14px range rather than the circle's 3.2px floor, because a 3px square has no corners left and the corners are the mark.
- **The chips are the legend, so they draw from the same table** (2026-07-26). `_map/glyphs.ts` also emits SVG, and the filter chips render it, so a chip cannot disagree with the mark it names. Before this they were all a 6px disc: `disasters` and `straits` were the same ring, and `markets` was a *grey* ring on the reasoning that the layer is olive and terracotta in equal measure so neither stands for it — true, and the conclusion does not follow. It shows both now, which is the one thing that chip could say that the reader had no other way to learn.
- **Off is an ink step, never opacity** (2026-07-26). An unlit filter chip was `--map-ink-muted` at `opacity: 0.5`, which composites to #484d56 on the ocean: **2.33:1**, under AA, on the label naming what the map is showing. `colour-system.test.js` could not see it because opacity is not a colour literal — which is why it survived. - **One pointer path, and it fixes a bug that was live for a month** (2026-07-30). - **The map opens on `population`, not press freedom** (2026-07-30). - **`worldFitZoom` fits the larger side, and the floor is desktop-only.** With `renderWorldCopies` off MapLibre refuses any zoom at which the world fails to cover the canvas in *either* axis, so the real floor on a portrait phone is the height. The old `max(1.35, …)` was three zoom levels above a phone's fit, and since that value is also `minZoom` the whole world was unreachable: the map opened mid-Sahara and had no gesture out. `homeCenterLat` mirrors `homeCenterLng` for the same reason — at the fit zoom MapLibre pins the centre to the equator, so comparing against the home latitude (21.4° since the camera moved to Makkah) left "whole world" lit before the reader had moved.
- **`Popup.addTo` fires `close` on its way in, and that must not read as a dismissal.** MapLibre's `addTo` begins `if (this._map) this.remove()`, and `remove()` fires `close` synchronously — so every re-render of an already-open card announced itself as the reader closing it. Since the `close` handler clears `pending`, and `open()` re-checks `pending` after awaiting the story, the check failed every time and the function returned before `setDOMContent`: **the story card never rendered a story**. It opened, said "Loading…", fetched the article successfully, and threw it away — no exception, no failed request, nothing in the console. `attach()` sets a `reattaching` flag around `addTo` so the handler can tell our own close from the reader's; every attach goes through it. **Better still is not to re-add at all**: an already-open popup needs nothing from `addTo` — `setDOMContent` has already run `_update` and moved focus — and what `addTo` adds is a remove-and-recreate that tears the card out of the document and puts it back a frame later, cancelling any animation running on it. `attach()` is a no-op while the card is up; the flag stays for the case that still re-attaches, a card the reader closed between the preview and the story.
- **The card opens once, at one width, and grows rather than jumps** (2026-07-28). Committing to a story used to walk the card through three geometries — measured on a 1440x900 desktop, `349x202` → `509x103` → `509x336`. It got 46% wider, **collapsed to half its height**, then more than tripled; the top edge fell 105px and shot back up 233px. Three causes. The **story fetch only started at `moveend`**, so an article shell holding one line sat between the preview and the story — and that shell is *shorter* than the preview it replaced, which is where the shrink came from; it is prefetched in `preview()` now, and since the flight is 1150ms against one small same-origin JSON that state no longer renders. The **preview and the article were sized apart** (20rem against 30rem) — they are one card at two densities and now share a width rule, so only height moves, which is the one dimension the content genuinely decides. And the remaining growth is **animated** (`growTo`, 220ms): `setDOMContent` applied it in a single frame while `_update` re-ran its anchor arithmetic against the new `offsetHeight`, so the card resized and repositioned together. The animation starts *after* the position is final, which is what makes it work rather than fight MapLibre — MapLibre's transforms are percentage-based (`translate(-50%,-100%)` for a bottom anchor), so a shorter box means a lower top edge and the card unfurls upward from its tip. Gated on `prefers-reduced-motion`, which also removes the flight, so those readers get the article in one step with no preview to replace.
- **Never write `setPadding` mid-flight.** `Map.setPadding` is `jumpTo` underneath and `jumpTo` calls `stop()`, so a padding write cancels the camera animation in progress. The drawer finishing its slide 200ms into a `flyTo` silently killed the flight and left the story's card open over a world view. Padding writes are skipped while `flying` and re-applied on landing (`writePadding`), redundant writes are dropped, and the drawer snaps shut rather than sliding when a selection is about to fly (`setExpanded(open, instant)`).

- **The land ramp has to be perceptible, not merely ordered** (2026-07-25). Shading the land by a metric only works if a reader can tell the quartiles apart, and the original ramp could not: adjacent stops measured **1.04:1** and the whole scale, worst country to best, measured **1.22:1**. Press freedom and urbanisation produced the same picture, so the picker felt broken. The cause was a self-imposed cap — the ramp was held under `border` (`#2b313b`) so frontiers would survive it — which was buying nothing, because that border measured 1.06:1 against the land and had already vanished. The fix moves the border up (`#5c6470`, now 3.3:1 against the ocean, so it draws coastlines properly) instead of holding the ramp down; the ramp spans **2.01:1** with every step at 1.15:1 or better, and saturation tapers with lightness so the top stop doesn't drift chromatic. `LAND_NO_DATA` sits near the ocean and is safe there *because* the border outlines every country — an unshaded country reads as an empty outline, not as sea. The night terminator (`#000` at 0.28) compresses the scale to ~1.64:1 on the dark half; that's the cost of having a terminator at all. **This was half the diagnosis.** "Press freedom and urbanisation produced the same picture" was read as a contrast problem and it was also a *distribution* problem — the percentile beneath it guaranteed the same picture at any contrast (see the next bullet). Widening the ramp was necessary and did not, on its own, make the picker teach anything.
- **The tone is the value, not the ranking** (2026-07-26). Every metric was placed on a *percentile* — position in the sorted order — which is uniform by construction, so exactly a fifth of the world landed in each fifth of the ramp on all twenty-seven. The histogram of tones on screen was therefore **identical whichever metric was showing**; only which country held which tone changed, which is most of why flipping through the picker taught a reader nothing. It also mis-calibrated in opposite directions on the two families: measured over the built payloads, the light half of the ramp covered **14.5% of the value range on literacy** (90%→100%, so Oman at 97% read as a different kind of country from Lesotho at 90%) and **99.7% of it on GDP** (the whole story from $72B to $27.3T crushed into two stops, while the dark half carefully separated $1B from $72B). `METRICS[key].scale` now says how each is read — `'linear'` for bounded indices and rates, `'log'` for counts, money and long-tailed rates — and the position is the projected value. Percentile was chosen to stop skewed metrics pinning at the floor, which is a real problem correctly identified; log fixes it without flattening ratios to adjacent ranks. The `scale` field is **editorial and required**, not derived: a new metric must state its scale rather than inherit a default that happens to be wrong for it. Zeros floor to the smallest positive value in the set, which puts Costa Rica's $0 military spending at the bottom of the ramp, where it belongs.
- **`ascending` turns the ramp around, and only it** (2026-07-26). The ramp meant magnitude on every metric, so the picker said "press freedom" while painting **Eritrea as the world's brightest example of it and Norway as its darkest** — and press freedom was `DEFAULT_METRIC` at the time, so it was the first thing every reader saw. The flag already means "lower is better" for the three metrics that carry it (press freedom, Gini, youth unemployment); it now flips the ramp too. The other twenty-four are untouched, deliberately: `population` has no better end and must not be given one, and adding the flag to `co2PerCapita` or `refugeesProduced` would silently renumber the ranks the country pages print. Which end is desirable is not inferable from a tone, which is why the legend now prints the value at **both ends of the gradient** — a bare 72px gradient is a scale with no units, readable only by someone who already knew the distribution, and prose cannot carry the direction once it varies by metric. `domain.dark`/`domain.light` are emitted from the same projection that placed every country, so the legend cannot disagree with the paint; they are read off the *numeric* extremes rather than off `p`, because the log floor ties at the bottom and the tie printed 1% for a ramp end a 0% country was also painted.
- **The country card answers the question the colour raised** (2026-07-26). - **The map's type is legible on the ground it actually sits on** (2026-07-30). The palette had been audited in one direction only: `colour-system.test.js` walks a 40-pair cross product of chrome ink against panel surfaces, and **nothing had ever asked about the text MapLibre paints**, whose ground is not a surface but a data layer that two separate things move — whichever metric shades the land, and the density wash over it. Measured, a **country name was 1.90:1 on the brightest land and 1.24:1 under the wash**; a city name 2.76 / 1.80; a sea name 1.92 / 1.25. The palette's own defence was that `labelHalo` carries them, and that is the error: **a halo makes a label findable, not readable.** You read a letter by its shape, and a 1.1px outline around an 8.5px glyph leaves the shape at whatever the ground allows. `labelDim` went `#727b88` → `#c5c9cf` (**4.90:1 / 3.35:1**), `label` `#8d97a6` → `#d3d7dd` (5.66 / 3.87), halos 1.1/1.2/1.0 → 1.6/1.5/1.4, and country names 8.5px → 10px, because contrast and size trade against each other and this was short on both. Two bars, since the grounds are not equally avoidable: AA on unwashed land, where most labels live, and the 3:1 non-text floor at the wash's peak, which covers a few dozen pixels around the busiest places on earth — going further would mean near-white labels or no wash. Pinned in `map-geo.test.js`, which walks all four inks against every ramp stop, `LAND_NO_DATA` and the ocean, washed and not.
- **The wash's ceiling is set by the type on it, not by how visible it can be.** `DENSITY_STOPS` peaked at 0.34 and the ceiling argument only required the wash to stay *darker* than the quietest ink — which is the condition for text to be invisible on it rather than readable, and the test asserted a 1.15:1 gap that a country name at 1.24:1 sailed through. The peak is 0.30 and the assertion is now the real one (3:1). Four points of alpha against a legible map is not a close call, and the same test's "lift" bar came down from 1.5 to 1.4 to record which way the trade went.
- **`label` and `neutral` were one value doing two jobs.** Both were `#8d97a6`, and the note on `neutral` still called that "the map's tone for furniture, not data". `neutral` is a *mark* saying nothing is happening — a 7px silhouette with a dark halo, read by its shape — and `label` is text. The argument was sound for the first and never sound for the second; sharing the value is what hid the bug, so a test now asserts they differ. `waterLabel` split off `water` for the same reason: everything the long note on `water` argues is about a *line* separating from `border` on a hatched country, and none of it is about a word.
- **Country names had no zoom floor problem on a desktop and were absent on a phone.** `country-labels` carried `minzoom: 1.1`, which is fine at the 1.11 a 1104px canvas opens at and hopeless at the **−0.39 a portrait phone** does — so the map had no country names at all at the one view every phone reader starts from. This is the `sea-labels` bug exactly, whose fix note already says density is the rank filter's job and never a zoom floor's; it simply went unnoticed on the more prominent layer for longer. The floor is gone and the area gate gained a first step: **0.006 sr below zoom 1.1**, which admits 75 candidates of which collision places about twenty, and `symbol-sort-key` sorts on area so the survivors are Russia, China, Brazil, Canada, the United States, Australia and their peers rather than whichever the index reached first. From 1.1 up nothing changed. A test asserts no label layer is floored above a phone's opening zoom, with `place-labels` the stated exception.
- **"No figure" is hatched, not shaded** (2026-07-25). On a ramp where lighter means more, *any* tone reads as a position on the scale — so a country painted below the floor is asserting "lowest", and for the ~30 countries absent from `country-augmented` (Saudi Arabia, the US, the UK, South Africa, South Korea, the UAE, New Zealand…) that assertion is simply false. The map drew Saudi Arabia, ~85% urban, as the least urbanised country on earth, and did the same on every other augmented metric. `literacyPct` covers 85 of 169 countries, so there it was making that claim about half the world at once. `nodataHatch()` in `_map/style.ts` registers a 45° 8px sprite and `land-nodata` paints it wherever feature-state is absent — a difference of *kind*, which no choice of tone can express. Registration is wrapped in try/catch and is the only thing so wrapped: everything added after it in `addDataLayers` is the actual data, and a texture must not be able to take the map down with it. **And the key draws the hatch** (2026-07-30): it was a flat `LAND_NO_DATA` square whose own `title` began "Hatched: no figure for this metric" — a legend naming a mark it did not draw, and of every mark here the one where that gap matters most, since the argument above is precisely that the tone *cannot* do this job. `#0d1015` is two points off the ocean, so against a ramp whose dark end is also near-black the reader had a dark chip beside a dark gradient and nothing to separate them. `NODATA_HATCH` is now the one constant the sprite is rasterised from and the swatch is styled from, reaching CSS the way `--c` and `--ramp` already do; the chip is 16px wide because at the tile's 5.66px period a square holds one and a half strokes, and one stroke through a box reads as a defect. Both swatch and ramp took `--map-line-loud` for their edge — at `--map-line`'s 1.42:1 a sample of near-black ground had no boundary, so the swatch read as an empty outline and the ramp as a bar starting a third of the way in.
- **Country coverage is a data gap, not a rendering one.** `shared/countries/country-augmented.ts` holds 144 of `country-data`'s 176 countries, and its header names a generator — `scripts/build-country-augment.mjs` — **that is not in the repo**, so it cannot be regenerated. The missing names are genuinely absent, not misspelled; they were checked. The United States has figures for **8 of 27** metrics for this reason. Until that script comes back, those countries are correctly hatched rather than wrongly shaded.
- **`populationDensity` is derived in `getMetricValue`, not read.** It is arithmetic — population ÷ area — and both inputs are native `CountryData` fields; it lived in the augmented table only because that is where the generator happened to compute it. So every country missing from that table reported no density while the site held both of its inputs. It is now derived whenever the table has nothing, which took coverage from 141 to 172 countries and gave the US, the UK, Saudi Arabia, South Africa and South Korea a figure. Stored values are never overwritten, so nothing published changed: France still reads 121, Germany 232, India 424 — the derivation was checked against them.
- **`CC_TO_TOPOJSON_NAME` is a join key, not a label.** `MK` was keyed `'North Macedonia'` — right for a label, wrong here, because Natural Earth 1:110m still says `Macedonia`. The join missed, the feature got no `iso2`, and the country became unshadeable on every metric *and* unclickable through to its profile — drawn, labelled and inert, with nothing thrown and nothing logged. `VU` was missing outright, same result. What the reader sees is `place-names.ts`'s job. A test now walks every Natural Earth feature and fails if one that has an ISO code cannot resolve to it; the five genuinely codeless features are enumerated, not tolerated.
- Solar, decay, basemap-geometry and built-payload invariants are pinned in `scripts/lib/map-geo.test.js`, which bundles the DOM-free modules with esbuild and asserts against them. **The ramp test now checks step contrast**, because the flat ramp passed every assertion there was — monotonic, neutral, under the border. Nothing asked whether the steps could be seen.
